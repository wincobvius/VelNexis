You sent crypto and it has been sitting unconfirmed — the wallet says "pending" and panic starts building. The most common cause is boring: the fee attached to your transaction was low, and the network is busy, so your transaction is waiting in the queue (the "mempool"). It is not lost — but how you respond determines how long the wait is.

## Check what is actually happening

1. **Find the transaction ID (hash)** in your wallet — every send has one.
2. **Paste it into a blockchain explorer** for that currency (blockchain.com for Bitcoin, etherscan.io for Ethereum, and equivalents for others). The explorer shows the truth: pending, confirmed, or not found — which tells you whether this is a network delay or a wallet problem.

## If it is pending — the fee queue

3. **"Speed up" if your wallet offers it.** Some wallets let you pay a higher fee to replace the stuck transaction (a "replace-by-fee" or "accelerate" button next to the pending transaction). This is the legitimate fix and worth using.
4. **Otherwise, wait.** When the network congestion clears, even low-fee transactions confirm — hours for Bitcoin in busy periods, occasionally a day or two for very low fees. Forced patience is normal here, not a malfunction.

## If the explorer shows nothing

5. **The transaction may not have actually broadcast** — that is a wallet issue, not a network one. Contact your wallet's support with the transaction details; do not keep re-sending blindly, or you may pay twice.

## The prevention habit

When networks are busy, fee estimates in wallets rise for a reason: paying the "fast" estimate avoids the queue entirely. On transfers that matter, take the recommended fee; the savings from a cheap fee are rarely worth the wait.
