You typed one wrong digit — and the money left your account to a stranger's account or wallet number. It is a sickening moment, but the outcome depends almost entirely on speed: money that is still sitting in the receiving account can often be pulled back; money that has moved on is much harder.

## Act in this order

1. **Call your bank or wallet service immediately** — the official helpline, with the receipt in front of you. Give them the exact amount, time, and the receiving account/wallet number. Ask them to attempt a **recall** on the transfer.
2. **The bank contacts the receiving bank** and requests a hold or return. This works best within hours, when the money has not been withdrawn or forwarded — another reason the first call cannot wait.
3. **Gather your evidence:** the receipt, transaction time, amount, the account number you *meant* to send to, and any messages with the intended recipient confirming the right number.
4. **If the receiver refuses to return it**, the matter becomes legal rather than banking: with your evidence, you can file a police/cybercrime report or a banking complaint — wrongful retention of a mistaken transfer is recognized in most legal systems.
5. **For wallet apps** (JazzCash, Easypaisa, and similar): use their in-app complaint option too — they have their own dispute channel and can freeze the receiving wallet while investigating.

## The prevention that actually works

For any big transfer: send a **small test amount first** (even 100 rupees), confirm the receiver got it, then send the rest. And read the account number back twice before confirming — most wrong transfers are typos, not system errors.
