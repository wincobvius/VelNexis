Your recovery phone number is gone — you changed your SIM, switched networks, or lost that old number — and now Google sends all security codes to a phone that no longer exists. Without updating it, you are one hack or one forgetful moment away from losing the account forever. Recovery is possible, and then the number must be updated.

## Getting back in

1. **Go to accounts.google.com/signin/recovery** and enter your email. When it offers to send a code to the dead number, click **"Try another way"** — that link cycles through every other verification method on the account.
2. **Use your recovery email** if you set one — codes go there instead.
3. **Answer from familiar ground:** continue the recovery on a device and location you have used before (your laptop, your home Wi-Fi). Google's risk system weighs this heavily, and recovering from a brand-new device often fails.
4. **You may be asked to confirm your account creation year, contacts, or old passwords.** Close-enough answers can pass; wild guesses do not.

## Once inside — do this immediately

5. **Update the recovery phone:** myaccount.google.com → Security → Recovery phone — enter your current number. While there, verify the recovery email too. These two fields are what stand between you and the next lockout.
6. **If your old number was recycled to someone else** (numbers get reused by telecom operators), updating it is not optional hygiene — it is urgent: whoever holds that number can potentially receive your verification codes.

## If every "Try another way" path fails

Submit the account recovery form from a familiar device and wait — Google reviews legitimate owners' requests, sometimes approving after a few days of retries from the same device. Patience and consistency (same device, same place) beat volume.
