You press the attach button and nothing happens, or the attachment uploads halfway and fails, or Gmail refuses with a size warning. Email attachments have a hard limit — 25 MB per email — that most people hit without knowing. Big photos, scans and videos cross 25 MB easily. The browser itself can also be the problem.

## Work through these

1. **Check the size first.** Right-click the file → Properties (or Get Info) — anything near or over 25 MB cannot travel by attachment. Gmail's way around it is automatic: it uploads to your Google Drive and sends a link instead. If that offer does not appear, do it manually — upload to Drive, right-click → Share → "Anyone with the link", paste the link in the email.
2. **Button does nothing at all?** Refresh the page and try again — a stuck browser tab silently kills buttons. Then try another browser: extensions (especially ad blockers and privacy tools) are famous for breaking upload dialogs.
3. **Upload starts and dies halfway?** That is the connection: switch networks (Wi-Fi to mobile data or vice versa) and try a smaller file to confirm. Corporate and public Wi-Fi sometimes blocks upload traffic specifically.
4. **One specific file refuses?** Its name may contain characters that upset the uploader — rename it to plain letters and numbers. Also check the file is not open in its editing app (a file locked by another program can fail to upload).

## The habit worth forming

For anything you will send more than once — a portfolio, a form, photos of a document — use Drive links, not attachments. Links never hit size limits, and you can update the file without resending anything.
