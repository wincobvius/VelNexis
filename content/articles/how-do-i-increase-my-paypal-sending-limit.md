PayPal caps how much unverified accounts can send or withdraw, and you have hit that ceiling right when you need to make a payment. The limit is not punishment — it is simply what unverified accounts get. Completing verification (proving your identity, bank and card) removes the cap, and each verification step raises it further.

## The verification checklist

1. **Confirm your email and phone number** — the fastest partial lifts come from these two. Look for the confirmation prompts on your account summary page.
2. **Link and confirm a bank account.** PayPal makes two tiny deposits (or a card charge with a code) and asks you to confirm the amounts — completing this is the single biggest limit raiser.
3. **Link and confirm a card**, with the CVV and the verification code your bank shows for the small test charge.
4. **Provide identity documents if asked** (the Resolution Center or profile "verification" banner will say exactly what is missing). A photo ID and a proof of address are the usual requests.
5. **Sell regularly? Consider a Business account** — same verification logic, but higher ceilings and features for sellers. You can switch from your existing account without starting over.

## Worth knowing

Limits lift gradually, not all at once — each completed item raises the ceiling. And if you hit a limit *mid-transaction*, the honest fix is finishing verification and retrying, not creating a second account: duplicate accounts get both limited.

## Quick recap
- Verify your identity fully - legal name, address, ID documents - most limits lift after that.
- Link and confirm a bank account, not just a card.
- Limits rise gradually with account age and clean activity - sudden large transactions slow it down.
- Never buy 'verified accounts' - they are stolen or fake, and permanent bans follow.
