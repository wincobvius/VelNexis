The app showed one exchange rate, but the money that arrived was converted at a worse one — quietly costing you real money. Before assuming you were cheated, know that two legitimate things happened in between: currency rates move every second, and most services add their own margin on top of the market rate. The difference between the two — a locked rate and a processing-time rate — explains almost every case.

## Why the rate changed

1. **The shown rate was a moment's snapshot.** Live rates move constantly; what you saw was real at that second. The question is what the service promised to honor.
2. **Some services lock the rate at transfer time; others apply the rate at processing time.** Transfer-focused services (Wise is the well-known example) typically show and lock the rate you will actually get. Banks and card conversions usually apply their own rate when the transaction processes — sometimes days later.
3. **The service's margin is built into their rate.** The "rate applied" on your receipt is usually not the mid-market rate — the difference is the service's earning. That is disclosed business, not theft, but it is why the same transfer costs different amounts at different services.

## Reading your receipt

4. **Find the "rate applied" and "fees" lines** on the transfer receipt. The rate applied times the amount, minus fees, should equal what arrived. If that math checks out, the difference was rate movement plus margin — legitimate, if annoying.
5. **If the math does not check out** — the applied rate differs from a rate the service explicitly promised in writing — complain with the receipt screenshot. Services that quote guaranteed rates honor genuine discrepancies.

## The prevention habit

Before any transfer, check the mid-market rate yourself (Google any currency pair) and compare it with what the service offers. The gap is the true cost — and comparing it across services is how you keep it small.
