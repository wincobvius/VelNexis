You try to log in and Facebook says your account has been disabled. Facebook disables accounts when it thinks you broke the rules — sometimes rightly (fake name, spam), and sometimes wrongly (real people get caught by mistake). Facebook offers an appeal form where you prove your identity with documents, and real users often win.

## The appeal

1. **Read the email Facebook sent** — it states which rule the disable relates to. Your appeal's wording should answer that specific accusation.
2. **Open the appeal form** (from the email's link, or facebook.com/help and search "disabled account appeal"). Log in when asked — a disabled account can still open its appeal page; that is by design.
3. **Upload a clear photo ID** — national ID card, passport or driving licence, with the name matching the account's real name. All corners visible, nothing cropped, colors natural. This is where most appeals win or die: a blurry ID reads as evasion.
4. **Write a short, factual appeal.** One honest paragraph: what the account is used for, why you believe the disable is a mistake, and any context (e.g., "my name is a common nickname of my legal name, shown on the ID"). No anger, no essays.

## What to expect

- Review typically takes days to weeks, answered by email. One well-made appeal beats ten rushed ones — repeated submissions can slow the queue.
- **"Disabled" vs "locked":** locked accounts ask for a security check and return quickly; disabled accounts need this appeal. The email tells you which one you have.
- **If the appeal fails,** the decision can be re-approached once with new information — but a final decision is final.

## Prevention

The most common triggers are names that do not look real, posting flagged links repeatedly, and sudden login from new countries. None of those is worth a decade of photos.
