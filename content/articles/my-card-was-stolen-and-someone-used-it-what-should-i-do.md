Your card is stolen — and worse, the thief is already using it, or you fear they will. Stolen cards are a race against time: every hour gives thieves more spending, while the banking system strongly protects victims who report fast. There is a specific order of actions that minimizes both the loss and the paperwork afterwards.

## The first hour

1. **Block the card immediately** — your banking app's card controls are usually faster than the helpline (look for "block" or "freeze" under the card). Blocked card, problem contained; everything else is cleanup.
2. **Report the unauthorized transactions** to the bank in the same session — by phone first, then confirmed in writing through the app or email. Note the reference number of your report. Banks can dispute and reverse unauthorized charges, and reporting speed is the strongest factor in your favor.
3. **Change passwords** for your banking app, and for any online stores where that card was saved — the thief may hold more than plastic.

## The follow-up

4. **File a police report** if the amounts are significant — banks sometimes ask for it during fraud disputes, and it strengthens the claim.
5. **Watch your statements for weeks.** Thieves often test with small amounts first, and some charges surface late. Report anything unfamiliar immediately, referencing your original report.

## What protects you

Card networks and most banks limit or waive liability for unauthorized transactions that are reported promptly — which is exactly why steps 1 and 2 cannot wait for a convenient moment.
