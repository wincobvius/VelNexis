Your game runs, but not smoothly — frames stutter, aiming feels heavy, fast scenes turn into a slideshow. That is a frame-rate (FPS) problem, which is different from network lag: lag makes your inputs arrive late, low FPS makes the picture itself choppy. Fixing FPS means making each frame cheaper for your hardware to draw.

## Drop these settings first — big visual cost, little gameplay value

- Shadows and shadow quality
- Volumetric fog, smoke, and ray-traced reflections most of all
- Motion blur (many players prefer it off anyway)
- Crowd density and extra physics detail

## Keep these where they are

- Texture quality mostly uses video memory rather than frame time — lower it only on genuinely small graphics cards
- Resolution is the most expensive setting of all. Dropping it one notch is the strongest lever when the others are not enough

## Make the improvement measurable

1. Turn on the game's built-in FPS counter, or your graphics driver's overlay, before changing anything.
2. Change one setting, replay the same spot, compare. That shows what your machine actually gained — no guesswork needed.
3. Cap FPS at your monitor's refresh rate. A steady 60 feels better than a jumpy 90.

## If settings barely help

The remaining bottleneck is hardware or background load: close heavy apps, check temperatures (an overheating GPU slows itself down), and update graphics drivers. On older machines, these routinely matter more than any in-game setting.

## Quick recap
- Shadow quality and ray tracing cost the most frames - lower them first.
- Resolution scale at 90 percent is nearly invisible and buys real FPS.
- Cap FPS slightly below your average for smooth, tear-free play.
- Fullscreen beats windowed, and a clean GPU driver install fixes stutter nobody can explain.
