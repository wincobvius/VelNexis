The printer makes its printing noises — but the paper never moves. It sits in the tray untouched while the printer "prints" nothing. The paper pickup roller (the rubber wheel that grabs sheets) is the star of this problem: it slips on fanned paper, refuses overloaded trays, and wears smooth with age.

## The tray-level fixes (free, 2 minutes)

1. **Fan the paper stack** before loading — straight from the packet, sheets stick together like wet glass; fanning separates them. Then square the stack neatly against the tray's edges and insert it fully.
2. **Respect the tray limits:** the little paper guide sliders must touch the paper's edges — too loose and the roller grabs at an angle, too tight and it cannot grab at all. And never overfill past the tray's max line.
3. **One paper type at a time:** mixed sizes or old damp paper in one tray feeds terribly. Humid paper (dry season in storage, or the opposite) feeds worse than fresh; a fresh ream often feeds where the old one slips.

## The roller fixes (free, 10 minutes)

4. **Clean the pickup roller:** the rubber wheel visible in the tray slot when you remove the tray — wipe it with a barely-damp lint-free cloth (water, no solvents). Dust-coated rubber is smooth rubber; cleaned rubber grips again.
5. **The worn roller verdict:** if cleaning changes nothing and the roller's surface looks shiny/polished rather than matte, it is worn — printer shops sell replacement rollers cheaply, and HP/Epson service manuals (public on their sites) show the 5-minute swap.

## The moisture trick (old-school but real)

For a worn roller you cannot replace today: a tiny dab of water on a fingertip wiped on the roller's surface restores grip temporarily. Not a cure — a bridge until the replacement arrives.

## The false pickup

If the printer reports "out of paper" WITH paper loaded, also check: the paper size sensor arm in the tray is not jammed, and — on printers with multiple trays — the job is not pointing at an empty tray #2 that the printer does not have.
