Friends report strange links and messages from your WhatsApp, Facebook or email — messages you never sent. Nothing is "sending by itself": someone or something is using your account. Either a hacker is inside it, or malware on one of your devices is posting automatically. Either way, the recovery sequence is the same, and it takes about fifteen minutes.

## The recovery sequence

1. **Change the account password immediately** — from a device you trust to be clean. If you reuse that password anywhere else, change those too, starting with your email.
2. **Sign out everywhere:** every service has a "log out of all devices" or "where you're logged in" page in its security settings. This evicts the intruder's session, which survives password changes until it expires.
3. **Turn on two-step verification** before you close those settings pages — it converts this from a recurring nightmare into a one-time annoyance.
4. **Revoke connected apps:** account settings → "Apps and websites" / "connected applications". Third-party apps granted long ago are a classic back door; remove anything you do not actively use.
5. **Warn your contacts** — a short status or message: "My account was hacked, don't click links from me." Your contacts trust your name; the warning protects them.

## The step that prevents the encore

If the spam keeps coming after all this, the problem lives on your *device* — scan it (Play Protect on Android, or reputable security software), and treat a factory reset of the suspect device as the decisive option, after backing up.

After regaining control, message the contacts who received the spam - one honest line prevents them clicking your compromised link. Then check sent folders everywhere: email, WhatsApp, Messenger. Scammers rarely use one channel only.
