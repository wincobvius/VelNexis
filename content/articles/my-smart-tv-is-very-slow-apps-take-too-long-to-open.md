Your Smart TV takes forever to start, apps open in slow motion, and switching between them freezes the screen. Smart TVs are small computers with weak processors — and over months they accumulate cache, updates and background apps until they crawl. A maintenance routine restores most of the speed; a streaming stick replaces the rest.

## The maintenance routine

1. **Full restart, not standby:** unplug the TV from power for a full minute, plug back in. Standby keeps weeks of accumulated state alive; a real power cycle clears it. Do this monthly — the single highest-value habit for TV speed.
2. **Clear the caches:** Settings → Apps → the slow app → Clear cache (wording varies by brand). The streaming apps' caches grow endlessly; a 2 GB cache on a 4 GB TV is a traffic jam.
3. **Uninstall the unused apps** — every installed app consumes storage and, on some systems, background attention. Be honest about what the TV actually runs.
4. **Turn off the "instant on"/quick start feature:** it halves boot time but keeps the TV in a permanently bloated state — ironic, but TVs run faster overall with it OFF and a clean boot.
5. **Check the internet speed AT the TV:** a slow app is often a slow connection — run the TV's built-in speed test or a YouTube "stats for nerds". Weak Wi-Fi at the TV's corner of the house looks exactly like a slow TV. A Wi-Fi extender or ethernet cable fixes the network version.

## The honest end of the line

TVs older than ~5 years with weak hardware do not magically speed up — the maintenance routine buys months, not years. The clean fix is a streaming stick (Chromecast, Fire TV, or similar): its hardware is newer than the TV's, apps run on the stick, and the TV becomes a dumb screen again — fast, cheap, and immune to the TV's aging software.
