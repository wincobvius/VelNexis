Your phone's gallery is your life's photo album, and it lives on a device that can be lost, stolen, dropped in water, or simply fail. Google Photos offers automatic cloud backup — every photo saved twice, phone and cloud. Setting it up takes minutes; the only decisions are account storage and quality.

## The setup

1. **Install Google Photos** (Android: usually pre-installed; iPhone: from the App Store) and sign in with your Google account.
2. **Turn on Backup:** tap your profile photo → Backup on. The app asks about quality:
   - **Storage saver:** slightly compressed, most photos count FREE against the 15 GB shared quota — the right choice for almost everyone
   - **Original quality:** pixel-perfect, counts fully against storage — for photographers and people with a Google One plan
3. **Let it finish the first sync** on Wi-Fi and charging (the setting exists for this) — thousands of photos take hours the first time. Watch the "Backup complete" line at the top.
4. **Verify before you trust:** open photos.google.com on a computer — your library should be there, browsable. A backup you have not SEEN restore is a hope, not a backup.

## The management habits

- **Free up space:** once backed up, Google Photos offers "Free up space" — it deletes phone copies the cloud already holds. Safe, but understand the trade: the photos now live ONLY in the cloud account — and if you ever lose the account, you lose the photos. For irreplaceable photos, a second copy somewhere (a computer, an external drive, another cloud) is the honest rule (see our backup-guide).
- **The quota reality:** the free 15 GB is shared with Gmail and Drive — our storage-full guide covers the cleanup when the day comes.
- **iPhone users:** also check iCloud Photos settings — running BOTH gives you two clouds, but watch double-storage consumption and battery.
