You clicked a link and the page that opened looks... wrong. Maybe a bank login that is almost right, or a "package delivery" page asking for something. The panic is understandable, but here is the honest scale of the problem: clicking alone is rarely the disaster. Modern browsers and updated phones stop most attacks at the click. The real danger starts when you **type something into the page** or **download and open a file**.

## The two-minute response

1. **Close the page now.** Do not enter a password, PIN, OTP or card details — no legitimate service asks for those via a link that arrived by SMS or email.
2. **Did you download a file?** Do not open it. Delete it, empty the trash/recycle bin, and run a full antivirus scan (Windows Security or your phone's built-in scanner).
3. **Did you type a password into that page?** Change it immediately — from the real site, typed by hand, not from the fake link. If you reused that password elsewhere, change it there too.
4. **Did you enter card details?** That is the fast lane: call your bank, block the card, watch your statements.
5. **Turn on two-factor authentication** on the account involved (and your email). Then this mistake costs you nothing even when it happens again.

## For the future

The habit that defeats phishing: never log in through links. Open a new tab, type the address (or use your bookmark), and log in there. Fake pages only work when you arrive the way they planned.

((ad))

If you entered nothing on the page, the risk is low - most attacks need you to type something. But if you logged in anywhere after that click, change that password now, before reading further.
