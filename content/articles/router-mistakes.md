Your internet plan might be fine — your router's *address* is the problem. These are the five most common placement mistakes we see, in order of damage.

## 1. Hidden in a corner or cupboard

WiFi spreads downward and outward from the antenna. A router stuffed in a cabinet loses half its signal before it even reaches your room. **Put it out in the open, and up high if you can.**

## 2. On the floor

Every meter of height matters. Floor-level routers blast signal into the ground and furniture. A shelf or table is the minimum; wall-mounted near the ceiling is ideal.

## 3. Next to metal, mirrors, or a fish tank

Metal reflects the signal, water absorbs it. Keep the router away from fridges, TVs, mirror walls, and aquariums.

## 4. In the far corner of the house

If your home is rectangular, the router belongs near the **center** of your usage area — not wherever the cable enters. One long ethernet cable fixes what years of "slow internet" complaints never did.

## 5. Next to interfering gadgets

Microwaves, cordless phones, baby monitors — all fight the 2.4GHz band. Even a few meters of separation helps.

## Quick checklist

- Out in the open, up high, near the center of the house
- Away from metal, water, and microwaves
- 5GHz for close-range devices, 2.4GHz only for range
- Still weak in one room? A cheap mesh node beats a "stronger antenna"

## Quick recap
- Router in a corner cabinet is mistake number one - center of the house, up high.
- Change the admin password from the sticker default - neighbors can log into old defaults.
- Restart it monthly and update firmware when offered.
- Same WiFi name for both bands makes phones cling to weak 5 GHz - split the names.
