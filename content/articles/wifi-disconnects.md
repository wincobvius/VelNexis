> **Note:** Some steps below reset settings or clear an app's data, so you may need to sign in again afterwards. Keep your passwords handy before you start.

Random Wi-Fi drops are the #1 Windows complaint we get. Work down this list — most people are fixed by step 3.

 

## The 9 fixes, in order

 

- **1. Restart router + PC** — boring, fixes 30% of cases
- **2. Forget & rejoin the network**
- **3. Disable power saving on the adapter** — Device Manager → network adapter → Power Management → uncheck “Allow the computer to turn off this device”
- **4. Update the Wi-Fi driver** (from your laptop maker’s site, not just Windows Update)
- **5. Switch router band** — crowded 2.4GHz channels cause drops; try 5GHz
- **6. Disable random hardware address** if networks misbehave
- **7. Network reset** — Settings → Network → Advanced → Network reset
- **8. Check for interfering apps** (VPNs, “optimizers”)
- **9. Router firmware update**

| Fix | Time | Success rate |
| Adapter power saving off | 1 min | Very high |
| Driver update | 5 min | High |
| Network reset | 3 min | Medium |

 

Q: Why does it drop only on my PC?
A: Almost always the adapter’s power-saving driver setting — Windows puts the card to sleep to save battery. Step 3 fixes it.

## Quick recap
- On laptops: Device Manager > WiFi adapter > Power Management > uncheck 'allow the computer to turn off this device'.
- Update the WiFi driver from the laptop maker's site - old drivers drop connections randomly.
- Neighbors' crowded channels cause evening drops - switch to 1, 6 or 11.
- Log the exact drop times - a pattern tells you whether it is hardware, heat or network.
