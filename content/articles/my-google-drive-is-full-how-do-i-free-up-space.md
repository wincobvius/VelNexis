Google's free storage is 15 GB — and it is shared by three hungry services: Gmail, Drive and Google Photos together. When the bar turns red, uploads stop, and mail can stop too. Most of that space hides in forgotten files: old WhatsApp backups, giant videos, trash that was never emptied. A guided cleanup usually frees gigabytes in minutes.

## How to fix it

1. Go to one.google.com/storage — it shows what is using space (Drive, Gmail, Photos).
2. In Drive, sort files by size and delete the big ones you do not need.
3. Empty Drive's Trash and Spam — deleted files still count until trash is emptied.
4. Delete old Gmail attachments: search "larger:10M" in Gmail.
5. Delete useless WhatsApp backups: Drive → Settings → Manage apps → WhatsApp → delete backup data.

## Free up Drive space fast
- Visit one.google.com - Google's storage manager lists big files, large videos and deleted items across Drive, Photos and Gmail on one page.
- Empty the Drive Trash and the Photos Trash. Deleted files still count against your 15 GB until the trash is emptied.
- In the Drive search box, type 'size:>10MB' to list the biggest files - delete or download-and-remove the heavy ones.
- Files shared with you also count: open 'Shared with me' and remove large folders you never open.
- In Gmail, search 'has:attachment larger:10M' and delete old heavy mails, then empty the Bin.
- In Google Photos, switch upload quality to 'Storage saver' - it shrinks future photos with almost no visible difference.

Run the storage manager at one.google.com before deleting anything manually - it lists the biggest files across Drive, Photos and Gmail in one place, sorted, with delete buttons. Ten minutes there beats an hour of folder hunting.
