You do not need to pay to try good AI. The main free options today — with the caveat that limits and availability change often, so check each service's current plan page:

## Where to start

1. **A general chat assistant.** ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Copilot and Claude all run free tiers strong enough for writing help, explanations and brainstorming. Free tiers cap how much you can use per day or hour, and the caps move often.
2. **Image generation.** Free usage exists inside several of the same assistants, and inside design tools like Canva's free tier.
3. **Code help.** The same chat assistants handle code questions well, and GitHub's Copilot has a free tier for individuals with monthly limits.
4. **Research and summarizing.** Perplexity's free tier answers with web citations — useful when you need sources, not just prose.

## How to use free tiers well

- Rotate between two assistants when you hit a daily cap, instead of paying for one.
- Never enter passwords, ID numbers or private financial details into any of them — free or paid.
- Treat every factual claim from any AI as a starting point to verify, not a finished answer.

Q: Are these really free, or just trials?
A: Genuinely free tiers with usage limits, at the time of writing — companies use them to introduce the product. Limits change frequently, so treat "free" as the current state, not a promise.

## Quick recap
- The big three - ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude - all have genuinely useful free tiers.
- Use free tiers to learn what AI is good at before paying for anything.
- Different tools suit different tasks - test the same prompt in two and compare.
- Free plans use your data by default for training on some services - check settings.
