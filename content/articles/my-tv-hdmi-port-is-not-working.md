One (or all) of your TV's HDMI ports stopped working — a device that worked yesterday shows nothing today. HDMI ports fail from worn connectors, bent pins, or board faults. The diagnosis is quick: the same cable and device in a different port tells you immediately whether the port or something else died.

## The swap test (2 minutes, definitive)

1. **Same device, same cable, DIFFERENT port.** Works → the original port is the problem. Still nothing → the problem is the device or the cable, and the TV is innocent. This one swap answers the question every HDMI forum thread argues about.
2. **Then same device, different cable** — cables are the cheapest thing to replace and the most frequent actual failure, especially cables that "worked before" at a lower resolution. A 4K signal needs a cable that carries it.
3. **Different device in the suspicious port** — confirming the port's behavior from both directions.

## If the port is the culprit

4. **Inspect with a torch:** bent pins, a cracked plastic tongue, or debris jammed in the port. A straightened pin (power OFF, gentle work with fine tweezers) sometimes resurrects a port — do not deepen the bend.
5. **Worn-but-working ports:** if the port works when the cable is held at an angle, the connector's grip has loosened — a repair shop can reflow/replace the socket; meanwhile, a cable with a fatter connector body stays put.
6. **Redistribute and move on:** most TVs have 3+ ports and one free — reassigning devices to healthy ports costs nothing. A broken HDMI port only needs repairing when ALL of them die together (that pattern suggests the port board, worth a repair quote against the TV's value).

## The ARC quirk

The port labeled ARC/eARC has TWO jobs, and a device working fine for picture but refusing sound-on-ARC is a settings problem, not a dead port: enable HDMI-CEC on both TV and device, and check the sound output routing (see our TV sound guide).
