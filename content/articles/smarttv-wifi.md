Your Smart TV refuses to join the home WiFi — it cannot find the network, rejects the password, or connects and drops within minutes. TVs are the weakest WiFi devices in most homes: their antennas are small, their software is slow, and their remotes make typing passwords painful. Most TV WiFi problems break down to five simple causes.

## How to fix it

1. Restart the router and the TV.
2. On the TV, forget the WiFi network and connect again with the password.
3. Check the password carefully — TV remotes make typing mistakes easy.
4. Move the router closer or use a WiFi extender — TVs often have weak WiFi antennas.
5. If the TV sees no networks at all, its WiFi module may be dead — a USB WiFi adapter or a cable connection can be the workaround.

Q: TV connects but video keeps buffering — same problem?
A: Related but different: that is usually weak signal or slow speed at the TV's location. An ethernet cable or a mesh node near the TV fixes it properly.

## Connect the TV properly
- Distance test: hold your phone where the TV is. One bar on the phone means the router is too far - TVs have weaker antennas than phones.
- Restart both: TV unplugged for a minute, router restarted.
- 2.4 GHz reaches further; 5 GHz is faster but shorter. Try both network names.
- Forget the network on the TV and rejoin carefully - TV password entry is where typos creep in.
- Update the TV software - WiFi fixes ship in firmware.
- Move the router even one meter, or change its channel, to escape interference.
- An Ethernet cable beats WiFi for a TV that never moves - permanent, fast, zero drops.
