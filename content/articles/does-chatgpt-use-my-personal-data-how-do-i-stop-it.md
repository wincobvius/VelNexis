People type surprisingly personal things into ChatGPT — work documents, health questions, code — and then wonder: where does this text go?

## The short answer

By default on the free tier, OpenAI says your conversations may be used to improve its models. You can switch this off: Settings → Data controls → "Improve the model for everyone." Business plans handle data differently — check your own plan's terms.

## What switching off does — and does not do

Turning the setting off changes what happens to conversations going forward. Do not assume it reaches back and removes earlier data from an already-trained model — that is not how model training works. Deleting a chat removes it from your history and, after the retention window stated in OpenAI's policy, from its systems. For exact timelines, read the current privacy policy rather than relying on summaries — including this one.

## The rule that never changes

Do not type into any AI chat what you would not share with a stranger's server: passwords, PINs, national ID numbers, card details, or other people's private information. Treat the chat box like a public bench, not a locked drawer.

((ad))

Q: Can humans read my chats?
A: OpenAI's policies have included limited human review of a sample of conversations for safety and training. Practices evolve, so treat the "public bench" rule as the safe assumption.

## Quick recap
- Settings > Data controls lets you turn off training on your chats and delete everything.
- Never paste CNIC numbers, passwords or client data into any AI chat - that habit beats every setting.
- Chats you delete may be retained for a short window - check the policy for current timing.
- The free and paid tiers follow the same controls - check yours today.
