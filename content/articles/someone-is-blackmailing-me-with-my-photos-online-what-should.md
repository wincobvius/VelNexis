Someone is threatening to share your private photos — demanding money, more photos, or both. First, the things that are true and worth holding onto: this is a crime, not your shameful secret. You are being targeted by people who run this as a business, thousands of people have been exactly where you are, and there are concrete steps that genuinely help.

## What actually helps

1. **Stop all contact-and-pay cycles: do not pay, and do not send anything more.** This is the hardest instruction and the most important. Payment and new photos do not end it — they mark you as someone who responds, and the demands escalate. Silence starves it.
2. **Preserve the evidence before blocking:** screenshots of chats, their profile, numbers or accounts, payment demands — with dates visible. This is what police and platforms act on.
3. **Then block them everywhere** — after evidence is saved. Do not announce it, do not have a last word.
4. **Report it:** in Pakistan, the FIA cybercrime wing (complaint.nr3c.gov.pk, helpline 1991) handles exactly this crime, and it is treated seriously. If you are elsewhere, your national cybercrime reporting site serves the same role.
5. **Use StopNCII.org** (free, run by a child-protection charity) if intimate images are involved: it creates hashes of the images so participating platforms — Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and others — can block them from being uploaded. You do not have to send the images to anyone; the tool is built for this.
6. **Tighten your accounts:** change passwords, enable two-step verification, and set profiles to private — the blackmail often starts from information scraped from open profiles.
7. **Tell one person you trust.** This form of crime runs on isolation and shame. A friend, a family member, a counselor — one informed person changes how survivable this feels, and how safely you act.

## What not to do

Do not negotiate, do not meet, do not try to "out-threaten" them, and do not delete your account in panic — the evidence goes with it.

Q: They said they already sent it to my followers. Is it true?
A: Very often it is a bluff — the threat is the product. But even if not, the steps above remain exactly the same: evidence, block, report, StopNCII.
