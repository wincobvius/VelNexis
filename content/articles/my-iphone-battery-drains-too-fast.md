Your iPhone used to last the whole day and now begs for a charger by noon. Battery drain has two families of causes: the battery itself wearing out (batteries are consumables, like tires), or software eating power (an app misbehaving, screen settings, background refresh). Apple shows an exact health percentage, so you can know which family your problem belongs to.

## Step 1 — Read the health number

1. **Settings → Battery → Battery Health & Charging.** "Maximum Capacity" tells you the family:
   - **Below ~80%** → the battery is worn. No settings change fixes chemistry; a battery replacement (a fraction of a new phone's price) restores the runtime genuinely.
   - **Above ~85% and still draining** → software is eating it. Continue below.

## Step 2 — Find the eater

2. **Settings → Battery → scroll the per-app list** for "Last 24 Hours" AND "Last 10 Days" — the 10-day view exposes apps that drain in bursts (background refresh, location). One app dominating that you barely opened is your culprit: update it, restrict it, or delete it.
3. **Screen time and brightness:** the screen is always the #1 consumer. Auto-brightness on (Settings → Accessibility → Display), and the always-on display off (if your model has it) are the two biggest settings-level savings.

## Step 3 — The structural settings

4. **Background App Refresh** → turn off for everything except messaging and anything you truly need live (Settings → General → Background App Refresh).
5. **Location Services audit** (Settings → Privacy & Security → Location): set most apps to "While Using" — apps pinging GPS all day are silent battery killers.
6. **Mail fetch:** Settings → Mail → Accounts → Fetch New Data — "Push" for the accounts that support it, longer fetch intervals for the rest.

## One honest note

After a major iOS update, elevated drain for 2-3 days is normal background re-indexing — judge the battery a week after the update, not the morning after.
