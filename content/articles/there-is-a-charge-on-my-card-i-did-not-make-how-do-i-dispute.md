Your statement shows a purchase you never made — a strange merchant name, a subscription you never signed up for, a duplicate charge. Card fraud and billing errors happen constantly, and the card system has a built-in weapon for exactly this: the dispute (chargeback) process. Acting within days keeps that process smooth.

## The dispute sequence

1. **Report the charge to your bank immediately** — the app's fraud option or the helpline. Reporting speed matters both for recovery and for limiting further fraud.
2. **Block the card and request a replacement.** If someone has your card details, more charges are coming; a new card number ends the series. Do this even while the dispute is pending.
3. **File the formal dispute/chargeback** — the bank gives you a form or an in-app process. State plainly: "I did not authorize this transaction" and give the date, amount and merchant name as printed.
4. **Attach your proof:** statement screenshot with the charge visible, and any evidence you were elsewhere (for large disputed amounts). You do not need to prove innocence — the dispute process asks the merchant to justify the charge, not vice versa.
5. **Note the timeline and reference number.** Banks often issue a temporary credit while investigating; final resolution typically takes weeks. Follow up through the same reference, not from scratch.

## Before disputing — the 60-second sanity check

Some "unauthorized" charges are legitimate charges under confusing names: billing companies for subscriptions (a streaming service billed via a payments processor), free trials converting, or a family member using the card. Check those first — disputing a real charge creates a bigger mess than it solves.
