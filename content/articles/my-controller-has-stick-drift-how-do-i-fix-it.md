Your character or camera slowly moves on its own even when you are not touching the controller — the classic "stick drift". Inside each analog stick, carbon pads wear down with use and start reporting tiny movements that never happened. Drift is the most common controller failure in the world, and it has fixes at every price level.

## Free fixes first

1. **Rule out software:** calibrate/reset the sticks in the console's controller settings (PlayStation: Controllers → calibration; Xbox: Accessories app; Switch: System Settings → Controllers → Calibrate). A recalibration cures mild drift caused by a confused zero point.
2. **Clean around the stick base** — compressed air or a dry brush into the gap where the stick meets the shell. Dust and crumbs sitting on the sensor pads cause drift-like symptoms; 30 seconds of air is the cheapest fix in gaming.
3. **Contact cleaner, carefully:** a tiny drop of electronic contact cleaner (NOT WD-40, NOT alcohol) under the stick cap, worked in with circles, then left to dry. This dissolves the grime on the pads and revives many drifting sticks for months. Use the cleaner made for electronics, with the stick clicked off and batteries out.

## The honest middle

4. **Dead-zone software:** on PC, tools like Steam's controller settings let you enlarge the dead zone — the range of motion the game ignores around center. It does not fix the stick; it hides the drift. For games where precision matters less, a free and instant solution.

## The real fix

5. **Stick module replacement:** drifting returns because the pads are physically worn — eventually every cleaning stops working. A repair shop replaces the stick module (a soldered part on most controllers, but a routine job) for far less than a new controller; DIY kits exist for the confident, and on some newer controllers the module is modular and replaceable without soldering.

## The prevention that genuinely works

Drift is accelerated by grinding the stick into its edges (hard pushes in fighting/racing games). Gentle inputs and not storing the controller with sticks pressed (under anything, in tight bags) slow the wear meaningfully.
