The internet works, then cuts for a few seconds, then returns — many times an hour. This is worse than a full outage because video calls and downloads break again and again. Causes include a loose cable, an overloaded router, old router software, a failing router, or a fault on the company's line. Watching when it drops (all devices or one, WiFi or cable) points to the cause.

## How to fix it

1. Check if the cable to the router is loose or damaged.
2. Restart the router.
3. Update router firmware from router settings.
4. If it drops only on WiFi but cable is fine, the router WiFi part may be weak. Try changing the WiFi channel in settings.
5. If it drops on all devices, call your internet company. It can be a line fault.

## Stop the drops
- Watch the router lights when the internet dies. If the line light goes red or blank, the problem is outside your house - log the times and call your provider.
- Replace or reseat the cable from the wall to the router. Old cables cause random drops that look like provider faults.
- Keep the router away from microwaves, cordless phones and thick walls - interference shows up as random drops.
- Routers that run hot all day drop connections at night. Give it airflow and restart it.
- Update the router firmware from its admin page (usually 192.168.1.1).
- If only WiFi drops but a cable stays fine, change the WiFi channel - a neighbor's new router may have landed on yours.
- Routers older than 5 years start failing exactly like this. Borrow one to test before buying.
