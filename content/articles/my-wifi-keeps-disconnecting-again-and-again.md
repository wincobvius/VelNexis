The WiFi works for a few minutes, then drops, then comes back on its own — all day long. This is very annoying for video calls, online classes and gaming. The causes are: weak signal because of distance or walls, too many devices on one router, old router software, or a power saving feature on your phone or laptop.

## The ladder, most-likely first

1. **Restart properly — the full triangle:** unplug the ROUTER for 30 seconds (not just the phone), restart the device, and if drops continue past a day, reset the router to factory only as a later resort. Routers that have been up for weeks accumulate connection-table garbage; the unplug clears it.
2. **Signal check:** where does it drop? Everywhere → router/network side. In specific rooms → distance and walls: move the router more central and higher (see our router-placement guide), or a Wi-Fi extender for the far corner.
3. **One device drops, others stay solid?** Then the problem is THAT device — and the usual suspect is power saving: Android: WiFi settings → Advanced → "Keep WiFi on during sleep" → Always; Windows: Device Manager → WiFi adapter → Power Management → uncheck "Allow the computer to turn off this device".
4. **Band congestion:** if your router splits 2.4GHz and 5GHz, 2.4GHz in an apartment building is a war zone of neighbors' networks — move your devices to 5GHz (shorter range, far cleaner). Routers that combine both under one name usually steer automatically, but old devices cling to 2.4GHz.
5. **Router firmware update:** the router's own settings page (usually under Administration/System → Firmware Update) — drops that began "for no reason" are frequently fixed by a firmware patch. Also check the router's age: 6+ year-old routers under modern device loads develop exactly this instability — replacement fixes what nothing else does.

## The pattern tells the story

Drops at the same time daily → interference or ISP maintenance. Drops when the microwave runs → 2.4GHz interference (real, and fixable by moving to 5GHz). Drops only on video calls → upstream saturation (someone uploading in the background). Matching the pattern to a cause is half the fix.
