Downloading from Google Drive crawls at a fraction of your internet speed — a file that should take a minute takes an hour. Google deliberately limits download speeds for free accounts and heavy users, and large shared files get throttled hardest. There are workarounds that help, and one golden rule about how to download big folders.

## What actually helps

1. **Download in smaller pieces.** A giant folder as one ZIP is throttled hardest and fails most. Select subfolders or individual files and download in batches — several smaller transfers routinely beat one monster transfer.
2. **Use Google's own apps where possible.** Google Drive for desktop streams files differently than browser downloads and often behaves better for large amounts. For photos, Google Takeout's archive (takeout.google.com) is the designed mass-download route.
3. **Try a different browser or incognito window.** Extensions — download managers, ad blockers, privacy tools — interfere with Drive's download flow in ways that look exactly like throttling.
4. **Check the real cause first:** run a speed test. If everything is slow, Drive is innocent — it is your connection, your Wi-Fi, or someone streaming in the next room.
5. **Pause and retry later.** Throttling on free accounts is time-based; the same download at a quieter hour can run several times faster.

## The golden rule

For anything important and large, never download through one browser attempt at the last minute. Use Takeout for exports, the desktop app for big syncs, and keep the original until the copy is verified.

Drive throttles large downloads by design during peak hours. For files above a few GB, pause other downloads, try early morning, and use the direct download link format that skips the preview player - it often triples the speed.
