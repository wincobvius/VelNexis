TikTok opens but videos refuse to play, the feed will not load, or the app freezes on the loading screen. Sometimes it is your internet, sometimes the app's saved data is damaged, and sometimes TikTok's own servers are down for everyone. The fix is usually one of five quick steps, and there is an easy way to check if it is down for the whole world.

## Quick checks first

1. **Check if TikTok is down for everyone:** search "TikTok down" or check a downtime-reporting site like Downdetector. If the whole world is complaining, do nothing — reinstalling will not fix TikTok's servers, and the spike on the graph is oddly comforting.
2. **Test your internet with another app** (open YouTube, load a webpage). If everything else streams, the problem is TikTok-specific; if nothing loads, fix the connection first — toggle airplane mode, or switch Wi-Fi to mobile data.
3. **Close TikTok fully and reopen it** — from the app switcher, not just the home button. Background apps freeze in broken states all the time.
4. **Clear the app's cache:** phone Settings → Apps → TikTok → Storage → Clear cache. (On iPhone, uninstalling and reinstalling is the equivalent.) Damaged cached data is the most common app-specific cause.
5. **Update the app** — an old TikTok build slowly loses compatibility with TikTok's servers, and the symptom is exactly this half-loading behavior.

## Still broken on one phone while other phones work?

Reinstall the app entirely, and check your phone's date and time settings — an incorrect clock breaks the security handshake that video streaming needs. And if TikTok is banned or restricted in your country, the app failing everywhere locally is policy, not malfunction — a VPN changes the legal situation, not just the technical one, so know which applies where you live.
