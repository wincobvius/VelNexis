Your external hard drive makes rhythmic clicking or beeping sounds — the "click of death" — and may not be detected. Inside every hard drive, delicate arms read spinning plates; when the mechanism fails, the arms click trying and failing to find their place. Every additional minute of running can grind the damage deeper. This is the one storage emergency where doing less is doing more.

## How to fix it

1. Clicking means the drive is failing mechanically. Stop using it now.
2. Every extra minute of running can destroy the data permanently.
3. Do not shake it, open it, or put it in the freezer (old myth — it damages more).
4. If the data is very important, professional data recovery services can open it in a clean room — costly but real.
5. If the data is not critical, try one quick copy attempt. If it fails, let it go.

## Clicking means stop
- The click is the read head failing to park. Every powered minute lowers recovery chances - unplug it now.
- Do not run checkdisk or repair tools on a clicking drive - they stress it further.
- Try exactly one thing yourself: a different cable and port. Still clicking? Stop completely.
- Do not freeze it, tap it, or open it - internet tricks kill drives, and dust ruins the platters.
- Professional labs recover clicking drives regularly, at a price based on the damage. Ask for a quote first.
- The decision rule: family photos or business files = lab; re-downloadable files = let it go.
- Next drive: keep two copies of anything important, on two different drives. Clicking arrives without warning.
