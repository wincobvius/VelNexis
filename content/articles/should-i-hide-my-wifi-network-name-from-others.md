You heard that hiding your WiFi name (SSID) stops hackers and freeloaders from seeing your network, and you want to know if it really works. Hiding is possible from the router settings, and after that your network will not appear in anyone's WiFi list. But it gives less protection than people think, and it can make your own life harder.

## What hiding actually does (and does not)

1. **What it does:** removes the name from the casual "available networks" list on nearby devices. Your network stops being visible to the neighbor scrolling their WiFi list. That is the entire effect.
2. **What it does not do:** hide the network from anyone actually looking. Every WiFi network, hidden or not, still broadcasts the frames devices need to connect — free scanning tools list "hidden" networks in seconds, showing the name, signal, and security. Against a targeted freeloader or anyone with a basic tool, hiding adds approximately zero.
3. **The real-world assessment:** casual neighbors who might ask for your password no longer see it — that is a genuine (small) benefit. Skilled attackers skip past it entirely.

## The costs people do not expect

4. **Your own devices work slightly worse:** devices connecting to a hidden network must actively probe for it ("are you there?") — which can reduce battery life, slow reconnection, and, ironically, broadcast your network's name in the probes, the exact leak hiding was meant to prevent.
5. **Guest friction:** every visitor needs manual network entry instead of picking from a list.

## The better defenses, in order of actual value

- **A strong WPA2/WPA3 password** (four random words or better) — the actual lock on the door
- **Router firmware updates** and a changed admin password
- **WPA3 if the router offers it**
- Guest network for visitors, if your router supports it

Hiding the name is security theater with mild side effects; a strong password is the real wall. Do the second, skip the first.
