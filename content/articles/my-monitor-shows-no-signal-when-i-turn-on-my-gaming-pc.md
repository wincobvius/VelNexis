You press power, the fans spin, lights come on — and the monitor says "No Signal". The PC seems alive but shows nothing. This classic has a shortlist of causes: cable plugged into the wrong port (motherboard instead of graphics card — the most common mistake), a loose cable, or displaced RAM from a move or bump.

## The checklist, most-likely first

1. **Which port is the cable in?** If your PC has a graphics card, the HDMI/DisplayPort must go into the CARD's ports (lower half of the back panel, horizontal row) — NOT the motherboard's ports (top, near USB). The motherboard port works only when there is no graphics card. This single mistake causes half of all "no signal" builds ever assembled.
2. **Reseat both ends of the cable** and the power cable to the monitor. Try another cable — HDMI cables fail semi-invisibly, and a borrowed cable answers in seconds.
3. **Try another port and another monitor/TV** (a TV with HDMI works). Works elsewhere → monitor or cable; same nothing → PC side.
4. **The RAM reseat:** power off, unplug, press the power button for 5 seconds to drain, open the side panel, remove both RAM sticks, and click them back in firmly. A stick half-out of its slot after a move or a bump produces exactly this: everything spinning, nothing showing. This is the most common FIXABLE hardware cause.
5. **Watch and listen for the boot beep/pattern:** some boards have a small status LED or speaker beep code — the manual translates it (memory vs graphics vs CPU). A "graphics error" code with the cable in the right port points at the card: reseat its power connector, then the card itself.

## If it was working yesterday and nothing moved

A monitor that shows its own menu (on-screen display) when you press its menu button is alive — the fault is PC-side. A monitor showing nothing at all, not even its menu, is having its own power/panel issue. This one test splits the universe in half.
