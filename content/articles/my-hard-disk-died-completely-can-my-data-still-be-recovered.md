Your hard drive is completely dead — no detection, no spin, strange noises, or just silence. Inside that metal box are your photos, documents, life. Whether the data is recoverable depends entirely on what broke: electronics (often repairable cheaply), mechanics (professional clean-room work), or the plates themselves (the data may truly be gone).

## How to fix it

1. If the drive is not detected at all and makes no sound — could be the circuit board; repair shops fix this cheaply.
2. If it clicks or beeps — internal damage; only professional recovery (clean room) works.
3. Software recovery only works when the drive is still detected.
4. Never open the hard drive yourself — dust kills the plates.
5. Decide by value: professional recovery costs a lot. Weigh what the data is worth.

## When a disk dies completely
- The honest rule first: if the files matter, stop powering it on. Every hour on a dying disk lowers recovery chances.
- Listen: clicking or beeping means physical damage - software cannot fix it and can make it worse.
- If the disk is silent and just not detected, try a different cable, a different USB port, and a direct SATA connection inside a desktop.
- Check Disk Management (Windows) or Disk Utility (Mac): if the disk appears with no letter, assign one - the data may be fine.
- A dead controller board (disk spins but stays invisible) is sometimes repairable by professionals with a board swap.
- Real recovery labs open disks in clean rooms. It costs money, but no home trick beats them on dead disks.
- If the data matters, go to a lab. If not, replace the disk and restore your backup - and start keeping real backups.
