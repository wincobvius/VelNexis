Your files suddenly open as garbage or refuse to open, a full-screen message demands money to unlock them, and a countdown ticks. That is ransomware — malicious software that encrypts your files and holds them hostage.

## What to do right now

1. **Do not pay.** Paying funds this industry and, critically, guarantees nothing — plenty of victims pay and never see their files again.
2. **Disconnect the machine from the internet immediately** — unplug the cable or turn off Wi-Fi. Some ransomware spreads to other computers and drives on the network.
3. **Do not delete anything yet, and do not "clean" the machine in panic.** The ransom note and encrypted files carry clues about which strain you are dealing with — and some strains have free decryptors.
4. **Identify the strain.** From a clean device, upload the ransom note (or one encrypted file) to the **No More Ransom** project (nomoreransom.org). If a free decryptor exists for your strain, this is where you find it.
5. **Assess your backups — on another device.** Backups on disconnected drives or cloud accounts may have escaped. Check them before anything else; they may make the whole negotiation irrelevant.

## If backups exist

> **Before you start:** Formatting the PC erases everything on it — only do this after your files are confirmed safe elsewhere.

Format the infected PC, reinstall Windows clean, restore files from the backup, then change every password you used on that machine, from a clean device.

## If no backups exist

This is the hard case: a professional cleanup (and possibly a data-recovery service) is the honest route — some strains are broken by researchers months later, so keeping an encrypted copy of files costs nothing. And whatever you decide: report it. Ransomware is a crime everywhere, including the FIA cybercrime wing in Pakistan (complaint.nr3c.gov.pk, 1991).

Q: Will antivirus unlock my files?
A: Almost never. Antivirus removes the infection; it usually cannot undo encryption. That is what backups are for.
