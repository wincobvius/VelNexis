You stopped receiving email, and people tell you their messages to you bounced back. That is what a full Google storage quota looks like: the free 15 GB is shared by Gmail, Google Drive and Google Photos together, and when it runs out, incoming email is rejected. It fills faster than most people expect because three services quietly eat the same pool.

The fix is to find which of the three is actually full first — guessing wastes time.

## Step 1 — Find where the space went

Open **one.google.com/storage** and look at the breakdown. If Gmail is the hog, clean Gmail. If Photos or Drive is the problem, no amount of email-deleting will help.

## Step 2 — If Gmail is full

1. In Gmail's search box, type `larger:10m` — this lists every email bigger than 10 MB. Old newsletters with heavy attachments usually top the list. Delete what you clearly do not need.
2. Search `has:attachment larger:5m` for a second, wider sweep.
3. Empty **Trash** and **Spam** — deleted mail still counts toward storage until you do. (They also auto-delete after about 30 days if you wait.)
4. Expect the freed space to show up within about a day; it is not always instant.

## Step 3 — If Drive or Photos is full

1. In Google Drive, open the **Storage** pane (left menu) — it lists biggest files first. Delete old videos and archives you forgot existed.
2. **WhatsApp backups live in Drive too** — often several GB. Know the risk before deleting: the chats on your phone are untouched, but if the phone is ever lost or reset, that history has no cloud copy left.
3. In Google Photos, switching future uploads to **Storage saver** quality stops new photos from eating quota as fast — it does not shrink what is already uploaded.

## Step 4 — If you would rather pay than clean

A Google One subscription adds storage for a small monthly fee. Prices differ by country — check the current price in the Google One app. But try the cleanup first: most people find several GB they did not know they had.

Q: What happens to email sent to me while my storage is full?
A: It is bounced back to the sender — it does not wait in a queue. Fix it quickly, and tell close contacts to resend anything important afterwards.
