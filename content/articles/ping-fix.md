High ping isn't one problem — it's a chain. Fix it in this order and you'll find your culprit in 15 minutes.

## Step 1 — Cable first, always

Wi-Fi adds jitter and spikes on top of your ping. A cheap ethernet cable beats an expensive router. If cabling is impossible, at least sit closer to the router and switch to 5GHz.

## Step 2 — Find who's eating the bandwidth

Pause every download, update, and stream in the house — a Windows update on a sibling's PC steals your packets' turn. Then test ping in-game again. On the router's app, check for devices you did not know were streaming.

## Step 3 — Measure where the delay lives

Run a ping test to your router (the first hop) and to the outside world. High ping to the ROUTER is inside your house: Wi-Fi, distance, interference. Normal ping to the router but high outside is your ISP's route — a different problem with different fixes.

## Step 4 — Pick the server next door

Game servers far away mean physics, not faults: 150 ms to another continent is the speed of light's opinion. Choose the nearest region in the game's server browser; a 100 ms improvement for one menu click.

## Step 5 — The DNS and VPN detour

A VPN usually WORSENS gaming ping (longer route) — but can fix a broken ISP route to one specific game's servers. Test it both ways rather than assuming. DNS (8.8.8.8) changes matchmaking lookups, not in-game ping — a common myth worth retiring.

## When it's not your network at all

Ping spikes exactly when the PC gets busy (loading, background tasks) point at the machine: check Wi-Fi adapter power saving (Device Manager → adapter → Power Management → uncheck "allow the computer to turn off this device"), close background launchers, and on laptops, plug in — power saving mode throttles the network card with everything else.

Q: What ping number should I expect?
A: Same country: 10-50 ms. Neighbouring regions: 50-100 ms. Other continents: 150-300 ms. If you are far above THAT baseline, something in this guide is yours to fix.
