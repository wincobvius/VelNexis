Someone else is inside your Gmail — you see strange logins, your password no longer works, or friends receive odd emails "from you". A hacked Gmail is the most dangerous account hack, because password-reset emails for your other accounts land in that inbox. You must recover it fast and then lock it properly so it does not happen again.

## Recover access first

1. **Go to google.com/accounts/recovery** and work through the verification — from a device and place you normally use (recovery from your own laptop on your home Wi-Fi succeeds far more often than from an unknown device). Answer with best-guess honesty; "roughly right" beats wrong-certainty.
2. **Once inside, change the password immediately** — myaccount.google.com → Security → Password. Choose something used nowhere else.

## Evict the intruder properly

3. **Sign out every other device:** Security → "Your devices" → detail on each unfamiliar one → "Sign out". A password change alone does NOT close an already-signed-in session — this step is the actual eviction.
4. **Audit what they changed:** recovery phone, recovery email, forwarding rules (Settings → Forwarding — a hacker's favorite trick is silently forwarding all your mail to themselves), and "Apps with account access" — revoke everything you do not recognize.
5. **Check the damage:** Sent items and Trash (what did they send or delete?), and connected accounts that used this email for password resets — anything important, reset those passwords too, from a clean device.

## Then lock the door

6. **Turn on 2-Step Verification** with an authenticator app, and save backup codes. This single step converts the entire nightmare into a one-minute incident next time.

Q: The recovery says my answers are wrong — what now?
A: Wait a few days and try again from the same familiar device. Repeated frantic attempts look like an attacker. One attempt per day from your home device, consistent answers, patience.
