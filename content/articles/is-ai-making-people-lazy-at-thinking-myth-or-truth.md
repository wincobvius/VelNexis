Students ask AI for homework answers, workers ask AI to write every email, and a worry grows: are we outsourcing our own thinking? Is the human brain getting weaker while AI gets stronger? This question has a real answer with two sides — AI can make you lazy or sharper, and the difference is in how you use it, not in the tool itself.

## How to fix it

1. It can, if you copy everything AI says without thinking.
2. Used rightly, AI saves time on boring work and you keep the thinking part.
3. Rule: let AI make the draft, you check, correct and decide.
4. Students should solve first, then check with AI — not the reverse.
5. The tool is not lazy — the user's habit decides.

((ad))

## A simple way to think about it
- Compare AI with a calculator. A calculator did not make people bad at math - but people who never learn the basics have nothing to check the answer against.
- The lazy part is not asking AI; it is accepting the first answer without thinking. Always ask 'why' and test the answer on a small example.
- Use AI to explain, not to replace. Ask it to explain a step you did not understand, then do the next task yourself.
- One honest habit: try to answer first, then ask AI. You notice your own gaps much faster that way.
- People who think with AI get faster; people who only copy from AI get weaker. The tool is the same - the habit decides.
- So the myth is half true: AI makes lazy people lazier and curious people quicker.
