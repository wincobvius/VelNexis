The ads all look the same: sleek earbuds, "premium sound", a price under $30. What can you realistically expect at that price — and where do cheap earbuds always cut corners?

This is a category overview, not a review of specific models — we have not lab-tested these, and honest buying advice does not need to pretend otherwise.

## What is genuinely fine at this price

- **Sound** for casual listening is surprisingly acceptable now. You lose refinement, not listenability.
- **Battery** on a single charge is usually adequate, with the case adding several top-ups.
- **Bluetooth basics** — pairing and indoor call audio — just work.

## Where the corners get cut

1. **Microphone** — usable in a quiet room, rough in traffic or wind.
2. **App and firmware** — clunky companion apps, rare updates, and features that exist only on the box.
3. **Durability and support** — expect a shorter life, and slow or absent warranty service.
4. **Fit consistency** — fewer tip sizes means your experience depends on your ears.

## The practical rule

If calls and workouts matter to you, the $35-50 tier from an established audio brand is usually where reliability starts. For videos and casual music, a well-reviewed budget pair is honest value.

Q: How do I spot the better budget pairs?
A: Look for reviews that mention weeks of real use, check the return policy before buying, and be skeptical of chart-toppers with no brand history.

## Quick recap
- Fit decides everything - try all tip sizes before judging bass.
- Test calls in a noisy place, not your quiet room.
- Use the return window honestly - two days of real use tells you everything.
- 'Burn-in improving sound' is a myth - what you hear on day one is what you get.
