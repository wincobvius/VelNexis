You need to send money to another country — family support, education, a business payment — and the costs are shocking: visible bank fees plus hidden exchange-rate margins that quietly take 3-7% of every transfer. The good news is that the market has modern, far cheaper options. The skill that saves the money is comparing the **total amount the recipient will receive**, not the advertised fee.

## Why banks are usually the expensive option

Traditional bank wires (SWIFT) combine three costs: a sending fee, sometimes a receiving fee, and an exchange rate marked up well above the real mid-market rate. The fee is visible; the rate margin is where the quiet 3-7% lives.

## The cheaper alternatives

1. **Dedicated transfer services** (Wise is the best-known example) typically charge a small transparent fee and use the real mid-market exchange rate — the one you see on Google. On a large transfer, the rate difference alone can save more than the entire fee.
2. **Remittance services** (Western Union and similar) still make sense when the recipient needs cash pickup rather than a bank deposit.
3. **Your bank, negotiated:** for regular large transfers, banks will sometimes offer a better rate than their posted one — it costs nothing to ask, and "priority banking" customers get this more easily.

## The comparison habit

Before any transfer, get a quote from two services for the same amount and compare **what the recipient actually receives** in their currency. That single number contains every fee and every hidden margin — no marketing can argue with it. Also check the delivery time that comes with it; the cheapest option is sometimes a day slower.
