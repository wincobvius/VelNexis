An email claims to be your bank, a delivery company or a prize, and asks you to click a link or share details. This is phishing — a fake email dressed as a real organization, designed to steal passwords and card numbers. These emails look very real now. Knowing the safe steps protects both your money and your accounts.

## What to do with it

1. **Do not click anything, do not download attachments.** Even the "unsubscribe" link in a phishing email is a trap — it confirms your address is live.
2. **Do not reply.** Any reply, including angry ones, marks you as a real responsive human worth more effort.
3. **Report it:** in Gmail, open the email → three dots → **"Report phishing"**. This trains Google's filter for everyone, not just you. (For plain spam — promotions, not fraud — "Report spam" is the right button instead.)
4. **Delete it after reporting.**

## If you already clicked or typed something

5. **Typed a password into the fake page?** Change that password right now on the real site — and everywhere you reused it. Then turn on two-factor authentication on that account.
6. **Opened an attachment?** Run a full antivirus scan immediately; attachment-borne malware often installs quietly first.
7. **Entered card details?** Call your bank and block the card — this is the fast-lane case, not the waiting one.

## The recognition habit

Real organizations never ask for passwords, OTPs or card details by email. And the sender address — not the display name — tells the truth: hover over it and read the actual domain. "HSBC Security hsbc.alert@secure-verify-hsbc.xyz" is a costume, not a bank.
