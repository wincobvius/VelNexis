You are leaving the Apple world — or just want your photos in Google's ecosystem — and need everything out of iCloud into Google Drive. Apple actually provides an official transfer tool for photos (privacy.apple.com), plus manual download routes. The golden rule of any migration: do not delete the source until the destination is verified.

## Route 1 — Apple's official transfer (photos and videos)

1. **Go to privacy.apple.com** and sign in with your Apple ID. Request "Transfer a copy of your data" → choose Google Photos as the destination.
2. **The transfer happens server-to-server** — Apple sends directly to Google, no download to your computer, no storage needed locally. It takes a few days for large libraries, runs unattended, and preserves albums' content (not their structure).
3. Requirements: your Google account needs enough free storage, and you will confirm the request twice. Apple emails when the transfer completes.

## Route 2 — Manual, for documents and everything else

4. **Documents:** iCloud.com → sign in → select files → download to your computer → upload to Google Drive. For Drive's app users, drag the downloaded folder straight into the Drive folder and let it sync.
5. **Photos manually:** iCloud.com → Photos → select (Cmd/Ctrl+A for everything) → download — then upload to Google Photos (photos.google.com), which organizes them automatically.

## The verification step nobody should skip

Spot-check the destination: open a handful of files, check the count looks right, check the oldest year of photos. THEN you may delete the iCloud copies. Until then, the source is your only real backup.

((ad))

Plan the move in parts - photos one week, documents the next. Bulk iCloud transfers tend to stall and fail silently, while small batches complete reliably and let you verify nothing was lost along the way.
