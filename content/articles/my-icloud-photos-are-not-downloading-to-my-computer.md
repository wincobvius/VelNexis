Your photos live safely in iCloud, but when you try to bring them to your Windows PC, nothing downloads — empty folders, endless spinning, or missing files. iCloud and Windows speak through Apple's official iCloud for Windows app; without it (or with optimization settings turned on), photos stay in the cloud on purpose.

## The proper setup

1. **Install "iCloud for Windows"** — from Apple's official site (support.apple.com → iCloud for Windows) or the Microsoft Store. The app is the bridge; without it, Windows has no supported way to pull iCloud photos.
2. **Sign in with your Apple ID** and approve the 2FA prompt on your iPhone/iPad — the approval popup on your device IS the sign-in code.
3. **Enable Photos (iCloud Photos)** in the app's options, choose whether to share new Windows photos upward too, and Apply. Downloads then begin into the folder you selected — the first sync of a big library takes hours, honestly, and shows progress in the app.

## When it is set up but still not downloading

4. **"Optimize Storage" on the iPhone is not the problem here** — but "Download originals" settings inside iCloud for Windows are: check Options → a full download needs enough disk space for the whole library.
5. **iCloud.com is the instant fallback:** sign in at icloud.com → Photos → select (Ctrl+A) → download — a genuine mass-download route when the app misbehaves, and often faster for one-time exports.
6. **App stuck spinning?** Sign out of iCloud for Windows, sign back in — it rebuilds the sync state. And check Apple's system status page (apple.com/support/systemstatus) when everything fails at once: iCloud outages look identical to local problems.

## One expectation to set

iCloud Photos is a sync, not a one-shot export — deleting a photo on the PC (in the synced folder or via iCloud) deletes it everywhere. "Downloading a copy" and "managing the library" are different jobs; know which you are doing before you delete anything.
