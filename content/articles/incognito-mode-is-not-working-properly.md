You use incognito/private mode expecting to be invisible — but websites still show your location, ads still follow you, or your office still knows what you opened. Incognito is the most misunderstood browser feature: it cleans traces on your device, but it never hides you from networks, companies or websites themselves.

## Reset the expectation, precisely

Incognito mode does three things: it keeps the session's history, cookies and logins off the device, and it deletes them when the window closes. It does not do three other things people assume: it does not hide your IP address from websites, does not hide your traffic from your internet provider, school or office network, and does not make you anonymous to your Google/Meta accounts if you log into them mid-session.

So "still seeing my location" and "ads still follow me" are not malfunctions — they are incognito working exactly as designed, which is less than people hope.

## What actually hides what

1. **Your IP and traffic** → visible to your internet provider and every site you visit, incognito or not. To change that you need a reputable VPN — which shifts trust from the ISP to the VPN company, so choose carefully. Free VPNs are often the worst deal in tech.
2. **Your signed-in identity** → if you log into Google or Facebook in an incognito window, you are fully identified for that session. Incognito is not a costume; it is just a clean slate on your side.
3. **Local traces** → this is the part incognito genuinely owns: a shared computer's history stays clean, and the next user cannot resume your logins. That, it does perfectly.

## Practical checklist

For local privacy: incognito ✓. For network privacy: VPN + DNS-over-HTTPS. For account privacy: log out or use separate profiles. Each layer is separate, and knowing which you need ends the disappointment.
