Your photos have gone soft and hazy, and you suspect the lens. You are probably right — camera lenses collect face oils, pocket dust and fingerprints that quietly ruin photo quality. Cleaning is completely safe with the right materials, and genuinely harmful with the wrong ones.

## The safe way

1. **Use a microfiber cloth** — the kind that comes with glasses. Never a tissue, napkin or shirt tail: paper fibers and fabric dust act like fine sandpaper on lens coatings.
2. **Breathe lightly on the lens** to fog it, then wipe in gentle circles. The moisture lifts oils; the circles avoid streak lines.
3. **For sticky grime,** put one drop of lens or screen cleaner on the cloth — never on the phone — and wipe. Liquid pooled near a lens can wick inside.
4. **Soft-bristle brush for the edges** — a clean paintbrush or makeup brush clears the ring where dust collects around the lens.
5. **Repeat weekly** if the phone lives in pockets and bags. Fingerprint haze builds back fast, and it is invisible until you wonder why every photo looks foggy.

## What never to touch the lens with

Household glass cleaners, acetone/nail-polish remover, alcohol-based sanitizers and pre-soaked household wipes — these can damage lens coatings and the surrounding finish.

## Quick recap
- Use a dry microfiber cloth in small circles - the same cloth as glasses works.
- For fingerprints, breathe on the lens lightly, then wipe - no liquids needed.
- Never use alcohol wipes or tissue on coated lenses - they slowly damage the coating.
- A dirty lens causes more blurry photos than any camera setting.

Keep a microfiber cloth in your bag or wallet - the one that comes with glasses works. A lens cleaned before every important shoot beats any camera setting, and it costs nothing.
