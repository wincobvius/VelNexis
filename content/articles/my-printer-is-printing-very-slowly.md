Your printer produces pages at a snail's pace — a page a minute when it used to do ten. Slow printing is usually a settings story: quality mode set to "Best", quiet mode on, or a weak WiFi connection drip-feeding the print data. Real hardware slowness exists too, but check the free fixes first.

## The settings audit (free, 2 minutes)

1. **Print quality:** in the print dialog → Printer Properties/Preferences → Paper/Quality tab → if "Best" or "High" is selected, switch to "Normal" or "Draft". Best mode passes the page multiple times — that alone can triple print time. Draft for internal papers, Normal for anything you hand to another human.
2. **Quiet mode off:** printers have a "quiet" setting that trades speed for silence (printer's own menu or its software). Quiet mode halves speed — exactly the symptom when "it got slow but I never changed anything".
3. **Duplex (double-sided) is slow by nature:** flipping the page mechanically takes time. Single-sided when speed matters.

## The connection audit

4. **WiFi printers at the edge of range** receive print data in drips: a document that would print in 30 seconds takes 5 minutes. Test with the printer closer to the router, or better, a USB cable — if cable-printing is fast, the wireless path is your bottleneck. A WiFi extender or relocating the printer fixes it permanently.
5. **The spooler queue:** old jobs queued ahead of yours (documents from days ago, waiting) print first. Clear the queue.

## The honest hardware limits

6. **Inkjets are slow at their best** — a 5-page document taking 3 minutes is normal for many consumer inkjets in high quality. Laser printers do this in 30 seconds; that is a hardware reality, not a setting.
7. **Photo printing is its own category:** photo paper + best quality = minutes per page BY DESIGN. Do not compare photo speed to document speed.
8. **The first page after idle is always slower** — the printer cleans/moves heads first. Judge speed on pages 2-10.

## One genuine red flag

Speed that degraded over MONTHS with no setting changes, plus growing noise = aging hardware (worn rollers, clogged heads repeating cleaning cycles). Multiple cleaning cycles before every print job is the printer fighting clogs — see our light-prints guide for the head-cleaning ladder.
