Movies show hackers cracking firewalls. Reality: **they log in with your password** — obtained by phishing, reused-password breaches, or SIM swapping.

 

## How it actually happens

- **Phishing** — fake login pages from “your bank”
- **Password reuse** — one leaked site unlocks ten of your accounts
- **SIM swap** — your number moves to their SIM, OTPs route to them
- **Malicious apps** — “free mods” with accessibility permissions

## The 4 habits that stop almost all of it

- **Password manager** — unique password everywhere
- **2FA via authenticator app** — not SMS where possible
- **Check haveibeenpwned.com** — know what leaked
- **Never grant “accessibility” permission** to random apps

 

Q: Is SMS 2FA useless?
A: Not useless — but SIM swapping defeats it. Authenticator apps (or passkeys) are the stronger choice.

## Simple steps that protect you
- Use a different password for your main email account. Email is the master key - anyone who gets in can reset your other passwords.
- Turn on two-step verification (2FA) everywhere it is offered. A code on your phone stops most attackers even if they know your password.
- Check if your email has appeared in a known leak at [haveibeenpwned.com](https://haveibeenpwned.com). If yes, change that password today.
- Never approve a login code you did not ask for. That is an attacker trying to get in - ignore it and change your password.
- Save your recovery phone number and backup email in every important account now, before you need them.
- If an account gets taken over, use that service's own 'recover my account' page. It works even when the password has been changed.

Do one five-minute audit tonight: list your important accounts, check which share a password, and split them. The single most common hack is one leaked password from a small site unlocking a big one.
