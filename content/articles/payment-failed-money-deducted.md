You paid online, the screen showed "payment failed" — but your bank message says the money left your account. Now the money is in nowhere-land: the merchant says no payment arrived, the bank says it was sent. Do not panic — failed transactions reverse automatically in most cases within days. Knowing the timeline and the proof to keep turns this scare into a routine wait.

## What actually happened

The payment never completed — but the bank's hold on your money did. Technically the money has not left your account; it is earmarked against an authorization that failed. Banks release failed holds automatically, usually in 5-7 working days (card transactions can take longer than bank-app ones).

## The waiting routine

1. **Screenshot everything now:** the failed message with date and time, the amount, and the bank's deduction SMS. This is your entire evidence file — one minute of work.
2. **Tell the merchant too** — send them the transaction ID. Their payment gateway can often see the stuck payment and push the release faster than the bank can.
3. **Check your statement after 2-3 days** for a "reversal", "refund" or "auto-reversal" entry.
4. **After 7 working days with no reversal,** contact your bank with the screenshots and transaction reference and ask them to trace the hold.

## When it is not a failed payment

If the merchant says the payment SUCCEEDED (order confirmed, service active) but the screen showed "failed" — then you made a successful payment with a lying error message. In that case the fix is with the merchant's support: the order exists, and a "failed" screen with a confirmed order means one transaction, not two.

Q: Can I speed up the reversal?
A: Not meaningfully — the release is a batch process on the bank's side. The merchant pushing from their end is the only real accelerator.
