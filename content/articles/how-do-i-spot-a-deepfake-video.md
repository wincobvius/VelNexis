A video is everywhere — a celebrity saying something shocking, a politician announcing something that would be huge news — and you cannot tell if it is real. Deepfake tools can swap faces and clone voices well enough to fool anyone briefly, including careful viewers.

The honest starting point: no visual check proves a video real or fake. Oddities are reasons to check further, not verdicts.

## Signs that justify a closer look

- Blurring or flickering around the face edges and hair
- Lip movements that do not quite match the words
- Odd hands, teeth, or lighting that does not fit the room
- A clip with no clear source, cut short, or missing its original context

These signs appear in many current fakes — and are absent from many others. Better fakes arrive every year.

## What actually works

1. Find the source. Is a known news organization or the person's verified account confirming it — or is the clip only circulating through random pages?
2. Look for independent reporting of the same event. One shocking clip with zero news coverage is a bad sign for something supposedly important.
3. Search the clip's key phrases — old real videos are often reused with new, false captions.
4. Before sharing something you are unsure about, wait a day. Delay costs nothing; spreading a fake harms someone.

Q: Can deepfake detector apps settle it?
A: Not reliably — they produce both false alarms and misses, like AI-text detectors. One signal, never a verdict.

Ask for a live verification - a quick video call where the person waves or says today's date. Every deepfake trick collapses in real time; that is why scammers always push for urgency and never for a call.
