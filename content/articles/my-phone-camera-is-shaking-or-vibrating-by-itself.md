Your camera makes a rattling or buzzing sound, and videos shake violently even on a stable surface. This is optical image stabilization (OIS) failing — the tiny moving parts inside the lens that steady your shots have been knocked loose or broken, often by a drop. It is a hardware fault with a clear repair path.

## Confirm what it is

1. **The rattle test (power off):** gently shake the powered-off phone near your ear. A loose OIS module rattles softly; a healthy camera does not. (A slight rattle on some models is the normal autofocus motor — compare with the same model if you can.)
2. **The video test:** rest the phone on a table and record 10 seconds of video. A healthy OIS steadies; a broken one makes the footage tremble as if filming an earthquake — that visible shake is the module vibrating.
3. **Open the camera and listen:** buzzing or ticking sounds when the camera activates are the OIS or autofocus motor stuck in a feedback loop.

## What helps and what does not

4. **No software fixes a loose OIS** — cache clears and resets do nothing for a physical module. Skip the app-level ladder this time; it is not that kind of problem.
5. **The temporary tricks** (worth trying, occasionally help briefly): power off for a few minutes; tap the phone camera-down gently against a soft surface (a mousepad, NOT a table) — a half-dislodged module sometimes re-seats. These are stalling tactics, not cures.
6. **The real fix is the camera module replacement** — a routine repair at any competent phone shop, moderately priced, usually same-day. Ask for the module, not "the phone needs servicing" vagueness.

## Until the repair

Shoot video with stabilization OFF in the camera settings (some apps let you) — an OIS fighting itself adds shake; software stabilization alone can look better. And back up your photos before any shop visit, as always.
