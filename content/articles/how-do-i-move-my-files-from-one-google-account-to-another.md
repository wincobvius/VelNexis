You created a new Google account — better name, school account, business account — and now years of Drive files sit in the old one. Moving between Google accounts has three routes: sharing (fast for folders), Takeout export (complete but slow), and ownership transfer (for shared files). Each fits a different situation.

## How to fix it

1. In the old account, share the needed folders with the new account (Share → enter new email).
2. In the new account, accept and use "Add shortcut to Drive" or "Make a copy".
3. For everything at once, use Google Takeout (takeout.google.com): export all Drive data as ZIP.
4. Download the ZIP and upload it to the new account.
5. Transfer ownership of shared files: Share → role "Owner" → transfer (works between Google accounts).

## Move files between Google accounts
- Drive: select files, Share with the new account, then log in as the new account and 'Make a copy' - the copies belong to the new account.
- For a full move, Google Takeout exports everything - mail, photos, drive - as files you upload to the new account.
- Google Photos: share your library with the new account, or download albums and upload them again.
- Gmail only: set the new account to check mail from the old one (POP3), or set the old one to forward everything.
- College and school accounts: download everything before you leave - institutions delete data quickly after accounts close.
- Moving ownership this way also frees the old account's storage.
- Do it in parts - Drive one day, Photos another - because large moves fail halfway and partial progress stays saved.
