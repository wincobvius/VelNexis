Bloggers everywhere heard rumors: "Google bans AI content" — and many sites using AI articles lost traffic. Others use AI daily and rank fine. What is Google's real policy? The confusion costs content creators months of wrong decisions. Google has stated its position clearly, and it is not what most people fear.

## What Google actually says

Google's guidance is consistent: it rewards **helpful, reliable, people-first content** — however it is produced. There is no rule against AI-written text as such. What its spam policies DO penalize: content made primarily to manipulate rankings — mass-produced, unoriginal, copied, or spun pages regardless of who or what wrote them.

## So why did AI-using sites lose traffic?

1. **They were punished for quality, not authorship.** The sites that crashed were mostly publishing mass AI text: thin answers, no expertise, no editing, hundreds of near-identical pages. That fails Google's helpfulness bar — the same failure hand-written content-farms hit in every previous update. The tool was new; the failure mode was ancient.
2. **The helpful-content system:** Google's site-wide assessment asks whether content demonstrates experience, expertise, and genuine value. A site where AI drafts were edited, expanded with real knowledge, and made genuinely useful sits on the safe side of that line; a site where raw output was pasted does not — the AI-ness is visible in the thinness.

## The practical policy for a site that wants to rank

3. **AI as a draft tool, you as the editor:** outlines, research directions, first drafts — then your knowledge, your examples, your verification. Publish what a knowledgeable reader in your niche would bookmark.
4. **The quality tests that matter:** original information or analysis, demonstrable first-hand expertise, appropriate depth for the topic, and editing that catches the confident nonsense AI produces (see our myths guide).
5. **The scale trap:** publishing 50 articles a day signals mass production to any system — gradual, real publishing wins (the same principle behind our own site's publishing pace).

## The honest bottom line

Google does not punish AI content; it punishes useless content. The safest position is also the simplest: never publish what you have not genuinely checked and improved — whatever wrote the first draft.
