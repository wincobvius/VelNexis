You deleted files from Google Drive — a wrong click, a cleanup that went too far — and now you need them. Drive is forgiving: deleted files rest in the Trash for 30 days, fully recoverable with two clicks. After 30 days, the rules change depending on your account type. Speed remains the deciding factor.

## Within 30 days — the easy case

1. **Open Drive → Trash** (or Bin) in the left menu.
2. **Find the file** — search works inside Trash; sorting by "Date deleted" shows your recent accidents first.
3. **Right-click → Restore.** The file returns to its original location, sharing permissions intact. Nothing else to do.

## After 30 days — the harder cases

4. **Workspace (work/school) accounts:** ask your Google Workspace administrator. Admins can restore deleted Drive files for their users for up to 25 days AFTER the Trash purge — a window most people never know exists.
5. **Personal accounts:** a deleted-and-purged file is generally gone from Drive itself. The remaining hope is elsewhere: a synced copy on a computer (the Drive desktop app may still hold the file locally if syncing was paused), a copy shared to someone else (ask them to re-share), or an email attachment of the original.

## Two habits that retire this problem

- Shared drives keep their own Trash with different retention — team files are worth checking there.
- For anything irreplaceable, "in the cloud" is not a backup strategy — keep a second copy elsewhere. Storage is cheap; re-creating a thesis is not.

Act within 30 days - that is how long Drive keeps deleted files in trash. After that, one final door remains: Google's recovery request form for files emptied from trash, which occasionally succeeds for recent deletions.
