Internet flies all day, then dies every night around 8–11 PM? You're not imagining it — and it's (mostly) not your router's fault.

## The real reason: neighborhood congestion

Most internet connections in a street share the same local line capacity. In the evening, everyone streams at once — so the shared pipe gets squeezed. This is called **peak-hour congestion**, and it explains 90% of "night slowness".

## The WiFi side of the problem

- 2.4GHz channels get crowded — your neighbors' routers fight yours
- Older routers auto-pick crowded channels and never change
- Microwave ovens and baby monitors blast the same 2.4GHz band

## What you can actually do

- **Switch to 5GHz** for your own devices — far fewer neighbors on it, and it carries more speed at close range
- **Move the router** out in the open, up high, away from walls and metal
- **Use a WiFi analyzer app** — pick the least-crowded channel manually
- **Ethernet for the important stuff** — gaming PC, work laptop: cable beats every WiFi fix
- **If it's the ISP line itself** (test with a wired speedtest at 9 PM): call your provider with the numbers — congestion complaints with evidence get taken seriously

Q: Will a new router fix night slowness?
A: Only if the problem is your WiFi (check: does a wired connection stay fast at night?). If the line itself is congested, a new router changes nothing — an upgrade or a different ISP will.

Q: Does a "mesh" system help?
A: It fixes weak coverage inside your home, not ISP congestion. Fix the right problem.

Run one speed test at 9am and one at 9pm, same spot, for three days. A consistent evening dip is congestion on the shared line - and that data, handed to your provider, gets plans upgraded faster than complaints.
