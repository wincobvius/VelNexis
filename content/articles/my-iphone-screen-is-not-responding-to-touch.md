You tap and swipe but the screen ignores you — or only some areas respond while dead zones ignore every touch. This has two very different causes: software confusion (fixed with a restart) or a damaged digitizer, the touch-sensing layer (needs repair). Drops, pressure in a pocket, and cheap screen protectors are the usual suspects.

## The 60-second triage

1. **Clean the screen and dry your hands.** Wet fingers, gloves, and screen grease make capacitive screens ignore touches that look perfectly normal to you.
2. **Remove the screen protector** — cheap or thick protectors, especially ones with bubbles or cracks, kill touch response. If touch returns with the protector off, you found a two-dollar problem.
3. **Force restart** — press volume up, volume down, then hold the side button until the Apple logo. This fixes the software side: a frozen touch layer after an app crash or an update hiccup. It is the single most effective step in this list.
4. **Test with Apple's diagnostics if touch allows** — or simply observe: do SOME areas work while a band or corner stays dead? A consistent dead zone is a hardware digitizer fault — software does not produce geography.

## If it is hardware

5. A dead zone, touch that drifts, or ghost taps — especially after a drop — mean the digitizer layer is damaged. Screen replacement at a reputable shop (Apple or a well-reviewed independent) fixes it; ask for the part's origin on out-of-warranty repairs.
6. **Before handing it over: rescue your data** while touch still partially works — back up to iCloud or a computer via cable. Repair shops are good, but your backup should not depend on them.

## The interim workaround

If touch is partly working and the phone is usable, enable AssistiveTouch (Settings → Accessibility → Touch → AssistiveTouch) — an on-screen home button and gesture menu that partially substitutes for broken areas while you arrange the repair.
