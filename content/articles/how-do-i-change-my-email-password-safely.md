You decided to change your email password — good security habit, or maybe a site you used got hacked and you are updating everywhere. The safe way is more than just picking a new word: a strong password has length and variety, and changing it should not lock you out of your own phone in the process.

## The change itself

1. **Go to myaccount.google.com → Security → Password.** You will be asked to sign in once more with the current password — that is normal verification, not a problem.
2. **Pick the new password well:** length beats cleverness — 12+ characters, a mix of capitals, smalls, numbers and a symbol. Four random unrelated words plus a number is both strong and memorable. Avoid anything guessable from your public life: names, birthdays, phone numbers.
3. **Use a password you use nowhere else.** A reused password means one leaked site eventually unlocks your email — the master key to everything else.

## Do this right after changing

4. **Update the password on your own devices** — phone mail app, tablet, computer — when prompted, so they do not lock out with the old one.
5. **While you are in Security settings, check two things:** that your recovery phone and email are current, and that 2-Step Verification is on. A strong password with no second lock is only half protected.

((ad))

Q: How often should I change my email password?
A: Current security advice: change it when there is a reason — a breach, a shared password, signs of trouble — rather than on a calendar. A unique, long password with 2FA enabled matters far more than frequent changes.
