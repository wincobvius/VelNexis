The budget phone market is the most crowded and the most misleading. Every phone claims a "great camera" and "all-day battery" — and half of them are lying. Before you pay, run this six-point checklist. It takes ten minutes and saves years of regret.

## The checklist

1. **Chipset first, always.** The processor decides how the phone feels after one year. Look up the exact chip name (e.g. Snapdragon 6-series, Dimensity 7000-series) — a strong mid-range chip beats big RAM numbers on a weak chip.
2. **Software updates: how many years?** A phone with 3-4 years of promised updates stays secure and smooth. Two years or "unspecified" means it ages fast.
3. **Battery: 5000 mAh is the floor, charging speed matters more than the number.** 18W charging feels ancient once you have used 33W or faster.
4. **Screen: Full HD (1080p), not HD+ (720p).** Budget phones cut corners here, and you stare at that screen 4 hours a day. Also check PWM/flicker complaints in reviews if your eyes are sensitive.
5. **Camera: ignore megapixels.** A 50 MP sensor on a cheap phone often loses to a good 12 MP. Look for actual photo samples in reviews, not the number on the box.
6. **Storage: 128 GB minimum.** 64 GB fills up in months and there is often no memory card slot anymore.

Q: Are "gaming" budget phones worth it?
A: Usually they just mean "same chip, better cooling and louder speakers". Fine if the price matches the regular model — skip if you are paying extra for RGB lights.

Q: Refurbished or new?
A: A refurbished flagship from a trusted seller often beats a brand-new budget phone — better screen, chip and camera for the same money. Just check the battery health and warranty.

## Why each item earns its place

**Chipset first, always.** A weak chipset makes everything slow forever — no setting fixes that. Look up the exact chip name plus the word review before buying; ten minutes of reading beats two years of waiting for apps to open.

**RAM matters less than storage size.** 4GB of RAM handles daily use, but 64GB of storage fills within months — and a permanently full phone is a slow phone. At the same price, prefer 128GB of storage over an extra 2GB of RAM.

**Update policy is the hidden spec.** A phone promised two years of updates stays secure and current; a phone with no promises starts aging the day you buy it. Brands publish update commitments — check before paying.

**Battery: trust reviews over mAh.** A 5000mAh battery with a hungry chip loses to a 4500mAh one with an efficient chip. Real-world battery complaints in reviews are the honest number.

**Test ports and buttons in the shop.** Plug in the charger, plug in earphones, press every key before paying. Port repairs cost more than most people expect on budget phones.

## Red flags in listings and shops

A listing that says minor scratches but shows no photo of the screen switched on is hiding something. A seller who refuses to meet at a mobile shop, or won't let you dial *#06% and match the IMEI to the box, is selling something else. Resealed boxes with the wrong charger are the oldest trick in the market. For imported sets, confirm the warranty actually applies in Pakistan — international warranty only usually means no warranty where you live.
