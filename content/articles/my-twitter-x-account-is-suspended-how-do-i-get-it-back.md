You open X (Twitter) and your account is suspended — your profile and tweets are hidden and you cannot post. Suspensions happen for rule violations (real or suspected): spam-like behavior, fake news reports, mass following, or hacked-account activity. X has an official appeal form, and accounts suspended by mistake are often restored.

## The appeal

1. **Read the email X sent** — it names the rule category. Aggressive following, fake engagement, and hacked-account behavior are the big three for ordinary users; your appeal should answer that specific accusation.
2. **Open the appeal form:** help.x.com forms page → "Appeal an account suspension". Log in with the suspended account — it can open the appeal form even while suspended.
3. **Write a short, calm, factual appeal:** what you use the account for, why you believe the suspension is a mistake, and any context (e.g., "I followed many accounts on my first day — I did not know that looks like a bot; it will not happen again"). Cut-and-paste outrage templates do worse than three honest sentences.
4. **If the trigger was automated** — you were hacked and the spam came from the intruder — say exactly that, mention that you have changed the password, and ask for review.

## What to expect

- Response time varies from days to weeks, by email. One clean appeal is enough; filing repeatedly slows everything.
- Some suspensions end on their own after a set period (the email says if yours has a time limit).
- A rejected appeal can be re-filed once with genuinely new information.

## Prevention, since reinstatement is never promised

The behaviors that trip the spam systems most: following hundreds of accounts in a day, liking/retweeting in bursts, posting identical text everywhere, and using "growth" tools. Nothing in that list grows a real audience anyway.
