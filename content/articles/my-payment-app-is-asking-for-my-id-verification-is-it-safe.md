Your payment app — a bank, wallet, PayPal, Wise, Payoneer — is asking you to upload your ID card or take a selfie for verification. It feels invasive, and the hesitation is healthy, because both things are true at once: legitimate financial services genuinely must verify identity (it is a legal requirement called KYC — Know Your Customer), AND fake "verification" requests are a favorite phishing trap. The skill is telling them apart.

## Telling the real from the fake

1. **Real KYC requests appear inside the app itself.** The honest test: do not use the link or message you received. Open the app yourself, from your own icon, and look — if there is a genuine verification banner or task in your profile or settings, the request is real. If nothing appears inside the app, the message you got was the scam.
2. **Legitimate requests never need your password or OTP.** KYC means documents and a selfie — it never means typing your password, PIN, or reading out an OTP "to confirm your identity". Anyone asking for those alongside documents is collecting keys, not verifying.
3. **Upload only through the official app or the typed-in-yourself official website.** Not through links in SMS, email, or WhatsApp — those lead to lookalike pages built for document harvesting.
4. **Check the timing makes sense.** Verification prompts normally come at logical moments: signup, a large incoming payment, a limit increase, or a regulatory update. A "verify within 24 hours or your account closes" threat out of nowhere, with pressure and a link, carries every phishing hallmark.

## If you already uploaded to a fake page

Treat your ID as compromised: report it to the platform the scammers impersonated, watch for accounts opened in your name, and be extra alert for follow-up scams targeting verified-identity victims.
