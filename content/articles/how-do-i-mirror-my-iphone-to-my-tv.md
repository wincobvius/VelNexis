You want your iPhone's screen on the TV — photos on the big screen, videos, apps. Apple's way is AirPlay, supported by most TVs from recent years. When AirPlay is missing (older TVs), brand apps and workarounds fill the gap. The two requirements never change: same WiFi and the right mirror button.

## The AirPlay way (built into most recent TVs)

1. **Confirm the TV supports AirPlay** (Apple's website lists models; most TVs from 2019+ do) and that the iPhone and TV are on the SAME Wi-Fi network — the number one "AirPlay not finding my TV" cause is two networks: the phone on 5GHz, the TV on 2.4GHz guest.
2. **Open Control Center** (swipe down from the top-right corner) → tap **Screen Mirroring** → select your TV from the list → enter the code that appears on the TV.
3. **For photos and videos specifically,** most apps offer the AirPlay icon directly (a triangle over a rectangle) — it sends only the video, leaving your iPhone free to use. Screen Mirroring sends everything, including your notifications — worth remembering before family viewing.

## When AirPlay is missing

4. **The TV brand's app:** Samsung (SmartThings), LG (ThinQ) and others offer screen sharing with phones in their apps — the quality varies, but for photos it works.
5. **A streaming stick ends the problem:** a Chromecast (with Google TV) or an AirPlay-capable device plugged into an HDMI port adds proper casting/mirroring to any TV with HDMI. For an older TV, this is the clean permanent fix rather than fighting adapters.
6. **The fallback cable:** a Lightning-to-HDMI adapter wired straight into the TV — zero lag, zero wireless fuss, ideal for hotel TVs and presentations.

## Troubleshooting the "not finding" moment

Same Wi-Fi verified, TV's AirPlay setting ON (Settings → General → AirPlay on the TV), iPhone restarted, router restarted. In that order — that sequence clears nearly every discovery failure.
