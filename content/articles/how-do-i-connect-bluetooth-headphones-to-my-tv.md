You want to watch TV with Bluetooth headphones — late night shows without waking the house. Modern smart TVs support this directly in their settings; older TVs need a small adapter. The pairing process is the same dance as phones, with one TV-specific menu to find first.

## If your TV has Bluetooth built in

1. **Put the headphones into pairing mode** — hold their power/pairing button until the light blinks (the same dance as pairing with a phone).
2. **TV settings:** Settings → Sound (or Sound Output / Devices) → Bluetooth → Bluetooth Device List / Add New Device. The exact menu name varies: Samsung hides it under Sound Output → Bluetooth Speaker List; LG under Sound → Sound Out → Bluetooth; Android TV under Remote & Accessories.
3. **Select your headphones from the list** — pairing takes a few seconds; TV audio switches to them instantly.
4. **To go back to the TV speakers later:** the same Sound Output menu → TV Speaker. The headphones stay paired for next time.

## If your TV has no Bluetooth (or claims not to)

5. **The Bluetooth transmitter adapter:** a small box (or a tiny 3.5mm/USB dongle) that plugs into the TV's headphone jack or optical port and does the Bluetooth for it. Cheap, works on essentially any TV with an audio-out port, and pairs once — the TV never knows the difference. For optical-only TVs, choose a transmitter with an optical input.
6. **Headphones via your streaming box:** if you use an external box (Fire TV Stick etc.), some support private listening through their app on your phone — audio routes to the phone, and wired headphones plug into the phone.

## The latency question

Cheaper Bluetooth routes can put audio a beat behind lips on screen. APTX Low Latency support (on both the TV/adapter and the headphones) removes the lag; if late-night dialogue is your main use, a small lag matters less than for action movies — but check for the feature when buying gear.
