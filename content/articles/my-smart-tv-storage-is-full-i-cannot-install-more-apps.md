Your TV announces "insufficient storage" the moment you try to install anything. TV storage is tiny by design — often just 4-8 GB — and a handful of streaming apps quietly fill it with caches and updates. Unlike phones, most TVs offer no memory card slot, so the strategy is: clean what is there, delete what you do not use, and when the TV is simply too small, move the brains to a streaming stick.

> **Note:** Uninstalling a TV app removes its sign-in, and formatting a USB drive erases everything on it — check what is stored before you format.

## Freeing the space

1. **Uninstall apps you do not use:** Settings → Apps → the app → Uninstall. Be honest about how many streaming services you actually open.
2. **Clear the cache** of the apps you keep — on most TVs: Settings → Apps → the app → Clear cache. This alone often frees surprising space.
3. **Delete downloaded files** if your TV stores media or has a browser with downloads.
4. **USB expansion, if your TV supports it.** Some Android TVs can adopt a USB drive as extra app storage — Settings → Storage. This formats the USB drive for the TV's exclusive use, which is why the note above matters.

## The practical upgrade

If the TV's storage is genuinely too small for your app habits, a streaming stick (Chromecast, Fire TV, or similar) plugs into an HDMI port and brings its own storage, its own app store and current software — the TV becomes just a screen. It is the fix that keeps working after every cleanup is forgotten.
