Your iPhone's speaker — calls on loudspeaker, music, videos — sounds quiet or muffled, even at full volume. The usual culprit is physical: pocket lint and dust packed into the tiny speaker holes, or a case muffling the sound. Software volume limits and genuine speaker damage come after that.

## The ladder, physical first

1. **Clean the speaker grilles** at the bottom edge (and the earpiece slit at the top) with a soft, dry brush — a clean, dry toothbrush works. Pocket lint compacts into those holes over months, and muffled sound is its signature. No liquids, no pins, no compressed air pointed point-blank.
2. **Remove the case** and test — thick cases and films cover the grilles on some models. If sound jumps without the case, the case is the muffler.
3. **Check BOTH volume sides:** the volume buttons, the Control Center slider, and the in-app volume (videos have their own). Also check Settings → Sounds — "Change with Buttons" on, and Ringtone volume up. A quiet ringer with loud media is normal — they are separate channels.
4. **Bluetooth sneaking:** if sound still seems quiet or odd, check that it is not actually playing to a paired earbud or car — Settings → Bluetooth, or the AirPlay/route icon in Control Center. A phone "quietly" playing into a dead Bluetooth device is a classic.
5. **Settings audit:** Settings → Music → Volume Limit (if set, raise or remove it), and Settings → Accessibility → Audio/Visual — the balance slider should sit centered; a dragged-off-center balance quietly empties one speaker.

## If cleaning and settings fail

Test with a song at full volume: distorted, crackling, or silent output after cleaning means the speaker unit itself is failing — a repair-shop part. And for the "calls are quiet but music is loud" case, that is the EARPIECE — a separate, smaller speaker with its own lint problem, same brush treatment.
