Someone else has taken control of your WhatsApp — they read your chats, message your contacts asking for money, or you got logged out of your own account. This usually happens through OTP tricks (someone asking for your 6-digit code), WhatsApp Web left open on another device, or spy apps. Speed matters a lot here — the faster you act, the sooner you get the account back.

## How to fix it

1. Log out of all devices: WhatsApp → Settings → Linked Devices → log out.
2. If you cannot log in, enter your number and get the code by SMS. The hacker gets logged out when you verify.
3. If the hacker turned on 2-step verification and you know the PIN, turn it off in settings.
4. If you cannot get the SMS code, email support@whatsapp.com with your phone number and write "lost access".
5. Tell your family not to trust messages from your account until you get it back.

Q: How do most WhatsApp accounts actually get hacked?
A: Almost always through the 6-digit OTP code — a hacker requests it and tricks you into sharing it. Never share that code with anyone, ever.

## Quick recap
- Open WhatsApp > Linked devices and log out everything you do not recognize - that is how hijackers hold on.
- Turn on two-step verification (Settings > Account) so re-registration needs your PIN.
- If you cannot register your own number, contact support from the app within the first hours - speed matters.
- Warn close contacts that messages asking for money 'from you' may be fake.

Speed decides everything in the first hour - log out unknown devices, enable two-step verification, then message your close contacts. The hacker relies on you discovering it late; remove that advantage.
