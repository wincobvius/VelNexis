A program you paid for suddenly announces "license expired" or "subscription ended" — though you believe you paid, or the date seems wrong. Before assuming theft or error: subscriptions do end quietly, renewals fail when cards expire, and a wrong system date makes valid licenses look expired. Sorting these in order finds the truth.

## The calm checklist

1. **Check the actual account (not the app's memory):** log into the maker's website — your account page shows the subscription's true state: active, expired, or payment-failed. The app's message can lag reality by days; the account page IS reality.
2. **The failed renewal:** subscriptions die quietly when the card on file expires or the bank declines the recurring charge. The account page shows "payment failed" — update the card, and the license revives on its own. Check your bank app for the declined attempt before concluding anything else.
3. **The system date check (one minute, free):** if your computer's clock is wrong — especially set to the past, or to a different year — valid licenses evaluate as expired. Enable automatic date/time and reopen the program. Phones and PCs with dead CMOS batteries drift dates and produce exactly this.
4. **Sign out and back in:** licensing servers refresh on a fresh sign-in; a stale token on the device claims expiry wrongly. The full sign-out (not just app restart) forces re-validation.
5. **The license key entry, for perpetual licenses:** non-subscription software asks for the key again after reinstalls or hardware changes — keep keys in your email or a password manager. Re-entering it revives the license; "activation limit reached" after a hardware change is fixed by deactivating the old machine (the maker's account page) or a quick support email — most makers reset counts on request.

## If the account page says "expired" and it is genuinely time

That is the honest answer: renew, or check whether the free tier covers your needs. And if the dates genuinely do not match what you paid for — screenshot everything and contact the maker's support with the receipt: billing disputes with evidence are resolved in your favor routinely.
