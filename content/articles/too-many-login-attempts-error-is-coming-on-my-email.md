You tried logging in several times and now Google blocks you with "Too many attempts" — and each extra try makes it worse. This lock is a security feature: it stops anyone (including you, and including a hacker guessing your password) from hammering the login. Waiting is the main medicine, plus a smarter recovery approach.

## The right response

1. **Stop attempting. Genuinely.** Every additional wrong try extends the block; the counter resets with time, not with effort. Most of these locks clear within an hour; some last up to a day.
2. **Try from your usual device and network** when you return — the one you normally log in from. The same account from a familiar laptop at home faces far less suspicion than from an unknown device.
3. **Use "Forgot password" instead of guessing.** A reset through your recovery options bypasses the attempt counter entirely — and if you genuinely forgot the password, this is the cure rather than the detour.
4. **Clear cache or use incognito** if the error appears even on the first try of a fresh session — a corrupted cookie can show stale block messages that are not really there anymore.
5. **If this keeps happening without you,** something else is happening: check Security → "Your devices" for unknown activity, change the password, and turn on 2-step verification. Repeated lockouts you did not cause can mean someone is running automated guesses on your account.

## The calmer truth

This error feels like being locked out of your own house. It is closer to a bouncer politely holding the door against everyone — including a thief wearing your coat — and letting you back in once the crowd calms.
