Your phone is behaving strangely — pop-ups outside any app, battery draining fast, unknown apps appearing — and you suspect a virus. The honest framing: true phone viruses are rare, because Android and iOS block most harmful behavior. What is common is **bad apps**: malware disguised as games, cleaners, "free VPNs" or adult apps, especially when installed from outside the official store. Checking takes ten minutes with tools already on the phone.

## The ten-minute check

1. **Play Protect scan:** Play Store → your profile icon → Play Protect → Scan. (On iPhone, there is no equivalent scanner — see the note below.)
2. **Uninstall anything you do not remember installing** — especially anything matching the bad-app costume list: cleaner, booster, flashlight, free VPN, or games that sideloaded from a browser link.
3. **Check what has scary permissions:** Settings → Privacy → Accessibility and Device admin apps. Malware's favorite trick is accessibility access — anything unfamiliar there gets removed, not just disabled.
4. **Remove device-admin rights first, then uninstall** — some bad apps protect themselves with device-admin status and refuse normal uninstall until it is revoked.

> **Before you start:** A factory reset erases photos, chats and files. Back up first — and afterwards install apps only from the Play Store, or the same problem walks back in.

## The iPhone note

iOS sideloading is locked down, so "virus" apps are far rarer. If an iPhone misbehaves, the cause is almost always an app from the App Store gone bad — delete and reinstall it — or a profile someone installed (Settings → General → VPN & Device Management: remove anything you do not recognize).

Q: Do I need an antivirus app on my phone?
A: Generally no. On Android, Play Protect plus careful installing beats most antivirus apps — which are themselves often ad-heavy and battery-hungry. Careful installing means: official store, real developer name, recent reviews.
