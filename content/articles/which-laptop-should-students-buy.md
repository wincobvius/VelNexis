Every student asks the same question: "which laptop should I buy?" — and every shopkeeper answers with the most expensive box on the shelf. The truth is that student laptops have a short, honest list of what actually matters. Skip the marketing, run through this checklist, and you cannot go wrong.

## The five things that matter

1. **RAM: 8 GB minimum, 16 GB comfortable.** Below 8 GB, browsers with 20 tabs and online classes start stuttering within a year.
2. **SSD, not a hard drive.** A cheap laptop with an SSD feels faster than an expensive one with an old hard drive. This is the single biggest difference in daily use.
3. **Processor: last year's mid-range beats this year's cheapest.** A Core i5 / Ryzen 5 from a recent generation handles everything a student needs — the ultra-cheap Celeron line does not.
4. **Screen and weight: you will carry this daily.** 14 inches is the sweet spot — big enough for notes and lectures, light enough for the bag.
5. **Battery: check real reviews, not the box.** Companies claim 12 hours; students get 5. Look for a review that mentions actual battery time, not the sticker number.

## What to ignore

- Touchscreens you will never use (adds cost, kills battery)
- Gaming graphics cards — unless the course actually needs them (design, 3D, video editing)
- "Free" laptop bags, mice and speakers bundled to justify a higher price

Q: Is a Chromebook good enough for students?
A: If everything happens in the browser — Google Docs, Zoom, PDFs — yes, and they are cheap and fast. The moment you need specific Windows software (SPSS, MATLAB, heavy editing), no.

Q: Should I buy last year's flagship or this year's budget model?
A: Usually last year's flagship. It keeps the better screen, body and speakers, and the speed difference in class work is zero.

## Match the machine to the field

**Computer science and engineering:** 16GB of RAM matters more than a fancy screen — compilers, virtual machines and containers eat memory. Any current mid-range CPU handles coursework; a dedicated graphics card only earns its price if the degree touches machine learning or game development.

**Business and commerce:** prioritize battery life, a comfortable keyboard and weight under 1.6kg, because you will carry it all day. 8GB of RAM is genuinely enough for spreadsheets, browsers and documents.

**Design and media:** the screen is the spec. Aim for 100% sRGB coverage before chasing any other number, and treat 16GB of RAM as the floor for video editing.

**Every other field:** an 8GB machine with an SSD, from a brand with a service center in your city, beats a better spec sheet from a brand you cannot reach when something breaks.

## Before you leave the shop

Check dead pixels first: open a full-screen white video and look. Type a full paragraph — one sticky key means a warranty visit later. Confirm the charger matches the rated wattage and the box seals were intact. On demo units, a battery report (powercfg /batteryreport in Command Prompt) reveals cycle count. Get the warranty terms written on the receipt — what is covered, where, and for how long. Five minutes at the counter saves weeks at the service center.
