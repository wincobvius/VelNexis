Your iPhone turns off and back on by itself — once a day, or in endless loops. Random restarts point to three suspects: a failing battery (very common on older iPhones), a software bug from a bad update or app, or hardware damage from a drop. The battery report and the restart pattern reveal the truth.

## Read the pattern first

1. **Restarts at 20-30% battery, or under load (camera, games)** → classic aging battery: voltage sags under demand, phone protects itself and reboots. Check Settings → Battery → Battery Health — below ~80% capacity fits this suspect, and a battery replacement fixes it genuinely.
2. **Restarts started right after an iOS update** → software. Check Settings → General → Software Update for a follow-up patch (Apple ships fixes fast when an update misbehaves), and update your apps — a crash-looping app drags the phone down with it.
3. **Restarts since a drop or water** → hardware, and no settings list will fix a cracked board. Service center, honestly, before the data becomes hard to rescue.

## The fixes to try, in order

4. **Force restart:** press volume up, volume down, then hold the side button until the Apple logo. Clears stuck states cleanly.
5. **Update everything** — iOS first, then every pending app update.
6. **The isolation test:** does it restart in safe conditions? Note WHEN it happens — on specific apps (delete/reinstall that app), on Wi-Fi, on charger, at low battery. The pattern is the diagnosis.
7. **Still looping? Back up NOW** (iCloud or computer) and try Settings → General → Transfer or Reset iPhone → **Erase All Content and Settings**. A fresh phone that still restarts is a hardware fault; a fresh phone that stops was software, and you restore your data from the backup you just made.

## The data-safety rule

Random-restart phones can die mid-ride: back up at the FIRST sign, not after the third restart.
