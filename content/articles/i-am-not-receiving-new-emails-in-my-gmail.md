People send you emails but nothing arrives — your inbox stays empty while the sender says "I sent it". You only discover the missing mails days later, and important ones (codes, bills, job offers) get lost. The usual hiding places: the spam folder, a full storage, or a filter quietly deleting or archiving incoming mail.

## Check the hiding places, in order of likelihood

1. **Spam folder first.** Gmail's filter occasionally misjudges good mail, especially automated mail (bills, codes, newsletters). If you find the missing messages there, open one and click **"Not spam"** — this teaches the filter and future mail comes through.
2. **Check storage: one.google.com/storage.** A full Google storage quota silently stops new email — the single most common "Gmail stopped receiving" cause people never suspect. Freeing space (delete big Drive files, empty Gmail Trash and Spam) restarts the flow within about a day.
3. **Look in All Mail and Trash.** A filter or a forwarding rule may be archiving or deleting incoming mail automatically: Settings → "Filters and Blocked Addresses" and Settings → "Forwarding and POP/IMAP" — check for rules you did not create.
4. **Open Gmail on a computer browser,** not the app. A syncing-stuck app shows old inbox state; the web version shows the truth. If the web shows mail the app does not, the app is the problem — clear its cache or re-add the account.
5. **Ask a sender to resend while you watch.** If it arrives in Spam or nowhere, you have learned exactly which failure it is — evidence beats guessing.

## The serious variant

If your storage was full for long, some senders' mail was **bounced back to them** — it never reached Google at all. Those specific emails must be resent after you free space.
