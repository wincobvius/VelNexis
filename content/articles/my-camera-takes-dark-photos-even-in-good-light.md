The room is bright, but your photos come out dark and murky anyway. Something is dragging the exposure down — and it is almost always one of five things. The exposure slider (check 2) is the fastest way to find out which.

## Five checks, quickest first

1. **Wipe the lens.** A fingerprint smudge dims and hazes every photo silently. This fixes more "dark photo" complaints than everything else combined.
2. **Set exposure on purpose.** Tap the screen where your subject is, then drag the small sun/slider icon up. If photos instantly brighten, some earlier tap had locked exposure to the wrong spot.
3. **Turn off filters and manual mode.** A beauty filter or a manual exposure value left set by another camera app sticks around and darkens everything. Switch back to Auto / Photo mode.
4. **Check HDR.** In mixed light (window plus room), HDR evens out dark areas — if it is off, indoor shots often look dimmer than reality.
5. **Reset the camera app's settings** from its own settings menu. This restores defaults without touching your photos — useful when nothing else explains it.

## If photos are still dark

Test the camera in another app (or the phone's other camera). A hardware sensor fault shows up the same dark in every app — that is a repair visit.

## Quick recap
- Tap the subject, then slide the sun icon up - most 'dark photo' complaints are exposure, not hardware.
- Clean the lens - a greasy lens cuts light dramatically.
- Try HDR on for backlit scenes and off for moving subjects.
- If everything stays dark in every mode, the sensor may be failing - compare photos taken months ago.
