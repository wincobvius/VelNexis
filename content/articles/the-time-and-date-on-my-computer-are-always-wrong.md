Every time you turn on the PC, the clock shows the wrong time or a date years behind. Wrong time sounds small, but it breaks real things: websites refuse to open (security certificates check the time), emails show wrong timestamps, and some programs stop working. The most common hidden cause is a tiny battery on the motherboard that has died.

## How to fix it

1. Settings → Time and language → Date and time → turn ON "Set time automatically".
2. Click "Sync now".
3. Check your time zone is correct.
4. If it resets every shutdown, the small CMOS battery on the motherboard is dead. It costs very little — a shop can replace it in minutes.
5. After replacing, set the time once and it will stay correct.

## Fix the clock for good
- Set the time zone first: Settings > Time and language. A wrong zone is why the clock 'fixes' itself to the wrong time after every sync.
- Turn on 'Set time automatically' and press 'Sync now'.
- If the time resets to a very old date every time the PC is unplugged, the small CMOS battery on the motherboard is dead. It costs very little and any computer shop can swap it in minutes.
- Change the time server to time.google.com if syncing fails with the default one.
- A wrong clock breaks website logins and causes 'your connection is not private' errors - fixing the clock fixes those too.

## Quick recap
- Set the correct time zone before anything else - wrong zone beats every sync.
- Time resetting while unplugged means the CMOS battery is dead - a five-minute shop fix.
- Switch the time server to time.google.com if sync fails.
- Fixing the clock also fixes strange login and certificate errors.
