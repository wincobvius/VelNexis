You need to send a huge file — a video project, a design pack, a movie — and email rejects it, WhatsApp compresses it, and USB drives are across town. Free file sharing is everywhere once you know the size limits: each service has a sweet spot, and the best choice depends on the file size and whether the receiver needs to download or just watch.

## How to fix it

1. Google Drive: up to 15 GB free — upload, right click → Share → Anyone with link.
2. WeTransfer: free up to 2 GB without any account — drag, enter email, send.
3. Telegram: "Saved Messages" allows 2 GB files and stays forever — very handy.
4. Dropbox: 2 GB free space.
5. For videos, YouTube unlisted link also works and streams instead of downloading.

## Easy ways to send big files
- Google Drive: upload, right-click, Share > anyone with the link. Free up to 15 GB per file and the receiver just downloads it.
- WeTransfer for quick one-off sends up to 2 GB - no account needed, and the link works for 7 days.
- Phone to PC on the same WiFi: Nearby Share (Android) or LocalSend (both Android and iPhone) - files go directly, no internet used.
- WhatsApp now sends files up to 2 GB - send it to yourself with Message Yourself.
- Compress huge videos first with HandBrake (PC) or the phone's built-in editor - often 70 percent smaller with no visible difference.
- Syncthing syncs folders between your own devices with no size limit and no cloud.
- Always send important files two ways - a link plus a copy on a drive. Links die, drives get lost.
