A text arrives with a login code — an OTP — for an account, a transfer, or a service you never touched. It is genuinely unsettling, and worth understanding, because the correct response depends on what is actually happening.

## What it usually means

1. **Someone is trying to log in as you** — they have your password and are waiting at the second step. The OTP arriving on *your* phone means the system worked: they cannot proceed without it.
2. **Someone is creating a new account with your number** (messaging apps, wallets) — the code is meant to verify it is really you. It is not, so do not share it.
3. **A scammer is "warming up" the number** — sending requests to see if someone replies or calls back asking about it. Any engagement gets you marked as a live target.

## The correct response

- **Share the code with no one.** No bank, no company, no "agent" ever needs your OTP — a request for it on a call or chat is the scam itself, every time.
- **Did you do nothing that explains it?** Then ignore it. Do not reply "STOP", do not call the sender.
- **Do OTPs keep arriving?** Someone is working on your account seriously: change that account's password immediately, check its security settings, and turn on two-factor authentication with an authenticator app.
- **Does the OTP mention money or your bank?** Call your bank's official helpline at once — rapid reporting is the strongest card you hold.

## The one habit to keep

Treat every OTP as private as your password — because functionally, that is exactly what it is.
