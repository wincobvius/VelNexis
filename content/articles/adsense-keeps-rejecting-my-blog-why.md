You applied for Google AdSense to earn from your blog's ads, and Google rejected it — once, twice, or five times — usually with the vague reason "low value content". Rejection is extremely common because Google's bar is high: it wants real, useful, original content before it pays you. Almost every rejection traces back to a short list of fixable causes.

## What "low value content" actually means

It is Google's catch-all for: not enough original value for a visitor. Concretely, reviewers (human and automated) look for:

- Thin pages — a few hundred words answering nothing deeply, or filler stretched around a keyword
- Copied or lightly-rewritten content — including AI text published without adding anything
- No clear purpose or audience — a general "tech blog" with no reason to exist
- Pages that exist to show ads, not to help a reader

## The fix list, in order of impact

1. **Audit honestly: would a stranger bookmark this page?** If not, that page is the rejection. Fix by rewriting the weakest 20-30% with real specifics — exact steps, real caveats, original angles. Depth beats volume.
2. **Thin-content math:** many sites get approved with 25-40 strong posts — but 100 thin ones fail. Delete or merge the weak ones; pruning RAISES the site's average value.
3. **The technical basics reviewers notice:** an About page, a Contact page, a Privacy Policy (AdSense REQUIRES it), a clean navigation, no broken links, no "under construction" sections. Missing legal pages are a silent rejection cause.
4. **Originality check:** run your key pages through a plagiarism checker — even honest sites pick up scraped copies of their own text ranking against them. And if you used AI drafts, publish only what you have genuinely edited and added to (see our AI-content guide for Google's actual policy).
5. **Wait for real traffic signals:** some patience helps — a site with a little organic traffic and comments reads as "real" to reviewers. Apply after you have a handful of genuinely useful pages indexed in Google.

## Reapplying the right way

Fix, wait two weeks minimum, reapply. Attach nothing, explain nothing — the site itself is the application. And keep publishing between applications: a site that grew since the last rejection tells a better story than a frozen one.
