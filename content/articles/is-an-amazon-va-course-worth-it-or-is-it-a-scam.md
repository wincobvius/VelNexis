Your social media is full of ads: "Become an Amazon Virtual Assistant, earn $1000/month, join our course for 50,000 rupees." The skill is real — Amazon VAs genuinely earn in dollars — but the course market around it is full of overpriced packages and false promises. Deciding between "real skill worth learning" and "expensive scam" needs a clear-eyed check.

## How to fix it

1. The skill itself (Amazon virtual assistant) is real and pays well.
2. The problem is overpriced courses — 50,000+ rupees for free knowledge.
3. Most of the content is available free on YouTube in longer, better detail.
4. Before paying any course, check the trainer's real student results, not just ads.
5. Better plan: learn free for 1 month, practice on a test account, then decide if you need a paid course.

## Judge the course before paying
- A real course sells a skill; a scam sells a dream. Watch out for ads promising thousands of dollars per month in 90 days - that income math is the bait.
- Check the teacher: do they run a real store or agency? Ask to see it. Someone who cannot show their own store is teaching from videos, not experience.
- Search the course name plus 'review' and 'scam' - honest courses have real student feedback everywhere.
- Learn the basics free first: Amazon's own Seller University covers the real tools. If free content makes sense to you, a paid course can deepen it. If it does not, paying will not fix that.
- Realistic start: VA work is a job, not passive income. First clients take months of applying.
- Never borrow money to pay for an online course.
- The honest rule: skills are worth paying for, guarantees are not.
