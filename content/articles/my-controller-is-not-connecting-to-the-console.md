Your controller will not connect — the light blinks endlessly, or the console ignores it completely. Wireless controllers lose their connection memory, run out of battery silently, or their Bluetooth pairing gets corrupted. The wired trick (connect by cable once) re-teaches the controller who its console is.

## The reconnect ladder

1. **Charge it first** — a "dead" controller with a silently drained battery blinks exactly like a broken one. Give it 15 minutes on a charger before any other diagnosis.
2. **The wired re-pair (the classic fix):** connect the controller to the console with its USB cable, press the pairing button or PS/Xbox button, and let it pair while wired. Once connected, unplug — the wireless link is usually re-taught by this single act.
3. **The pairing buttons:** every console has a small pair/sync button on the console itself AND a recessed one on the controller (back or inside the L2 area). Press the console's sync button, then the controller's, and let them find each other — this is the full re-pair for a controller that forgot everything.
4. **Restart the console** — controller connections are remembered by the console's Bluetooth stack, and a reboot clears stuck states.
5. **Distance and interference test:** pair from 30 cm away, in line of sight. A console buried inside a TV cabinet with a steel door fails at wireless everything; a USB extension or moving the console fixes it permanently.

## If it still refuses

6. **Test the controller on another device** (a PC with Bluetooth, a friend's console). Works there → the console's Bluetooth module (a repair); works nowhere → the controller's radio (controller repair/replacement).
7. **Cable trick as diagnosis:** if the controller works WIRED but never wireless, the battery or radio is the fault; if it works nowhere at all, the controller board.

## Multi-controller mixup

After parties and borrowed controllers, the console sometimes holds ghost pairings — unpair forgotten controllers from the console's accessories list, then pair yours fresh.
