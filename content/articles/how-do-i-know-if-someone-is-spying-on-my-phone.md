Your phone feels watched: the battery drains while idle, it runs warm at night, data usage jumps without explanation. Is someone actually spying — or is the phone just old?

## The honest signs of spyware

- Battery and heat: consistent drain while the phone sits untouched
- Data usage: steady background upload you cannot explain
- Unknown apps with generic names, or a "system" app behaving oddly
- Settings you did not change: accessibility permissions granted to something unfamiliar

None of these alone proves spying — a dying battery mimics all of them. Together, they justify a closer look.

## Who can actually spy, and how

Most commercial spy apps are installed **physically** — by someone who had your unlocked phone for a few minutes. That is why the people around us, not distant hackers, are the realistic threat. Remote installation through a "interesting APK" from outside the Play Store is the other route.

## The checks, in order

1. **Review installed apps** (Settings → Apps): uninstall anything you cannot identify, especially anything with "monitor", "family", "track" or a blank icon.
2. **Audit permissions:** Settings → Privacy → check which apps have accessibility, device-admin or microphone access — and revoke anything that has no business with it.
3. **Update and restart.** Updates close known spyware holes, and a restart interrupts many lightweight spy apps.
4. **Still suspicious after all that?** The reliable ending is a factory reset — it erases everything including spyware:

> **Before you start:** A factory reset erases all photos, chats and files on the phone. Back up what matters first, and do not reinstall apps from untrusted sources — that is often how the spyware returned.

After the reset, set a new PIN nobody else knows, and keep the phone locked by default.
