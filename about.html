Our Editorial Standards

Every article is written to be useful first: step-by-step instructions, sources for claims, and a clear "myth vs reality" verdict where rumors are involved. If I'm unsure about something, I say so — guesses never get dressed up as facts here.

How Every Guide Is Verified

Nothing goes live on reputation alone. Fix steps are checked against official documentation — Microsoft, Google, HP and Mozilla among others — and linked where the source matters. Rumor verdicts show the checkable evidence, not vibes. And when a guide turns out to be wrong, it gets corrected and the change is noted on the page. The full rules live in the editorial policy.

How a Rumor Gets Checked

Viral claims move faster than facts, so every rumor piece follows the same four steps:

  1. Find the source. Where did the claim start — a tweet, a YouTube video, a forwarded message? Half of all viral tech claims die at this step, because there was never a source.
  2. Check what the technology actually does. Does "AI reads your WhatsApp" survive the fact that WhatsApp chats are end-to-end encrypted? This is where a computer science background earns its keep.
  3. Look for the motivation. Who benefits if you believe this — the app asking for access, the channel selling fear, the "fix" that costs money?
  4. Give a plain verdict. True, false, or partly true — with the reasoning in view, not hidden behind jargon.

What We Don't Do

  • No sponsored verdicts. If a product is called good, that opinion was not bought.
  • No fake testing claims. Articles don't pretend hardware was benchmarked in a lab.
  • No scare tactics. Privacy and security matter, but fear is not a content strategy.
  • No content farms. Guides are written for readers, not for search engines to choke on.

Who Reads VelNexis

Mostly people with a problem and ten minutes. A student whose laptop won't boot before an assignment deadline. A parent fixing the family printer for the third time. A small seller whose account got suspended and needs the appeal process explained without legal language. If that sounds like you, you are in the right place.
