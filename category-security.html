Privacy & Hacking

Privacy guides, hacking explained simply, and security that actually works.

Most security advice is noise. This section keeps to the threats that actually reach people in Pakistan: phishing calls and prize scams, OTP fraud, fake apps that harvest data, ransomware, and the account-compromised panic message. Each guide explains how the attack works — you cannot spot a trick you don't understand — then the concrete steps: freezing a card, recovering a hacked Instagram, spotting a fake ChatGPT app, checking whether your email appeared in a leak. The advice is boring on purpose, because boring is what actually protects people.

Read these guides before you need them. The five minutes spent learning what an OTP scam sounds like, or what a fake ChatGPT app asks for, is the cheapest security that exists. If you are reading in panic — card typed, account hacked, ransomware on screen — open that guide first; each one starts with the single most urgent step, then works down the list calmly. One more habit worth building: a quarterly fifteen-minute check of which apps hold which permissions, and which old accounts still exist in your name. Every guide here gets easier when the attack surface is smaller to begin with. One last habit: when a breach makes the news, change that password everywhere you reused it — not just on the site that got hit. Reuse is how one leaked database becomes five broken accounts, and it is the single most common way readers end up in the hacked-account guide above.
