I clicked a fake payment link and lost money, what should I do

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Sep 1, 2026 · 3 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Sep 1, 2026

A link — from SMS, WhatsApp, a fake website — tricked you into "verifying" or "completing" a payment, and your money is gone. Online payment fraud is a race: banks can sometimes freeze or reverse a transfer if told immediately, while the money still sits in the receiving account. This is one situation where the next 60 minutes matter more than the next 60 days of regret.

The first hour, in order

  1. Call your bank's fraud line now — the official helpline, never a number from the message you received. Report the exact transaction, amount and time. Ask two things explicitly: can they block the card or account involved, and can they attempt a transaction recall.
  2. Follow up in writing (the bank app's chat or email) so there is a timestamped record of your report. Banks ask for this when disputing.
  3. Preserve everything: the link, the message it came from, screenshots, the payment confirmation, merchant names. Do not delete the conversation — it is evidence.
  4. Report to the cybercrime authority: in Pakistan, the FIA cybercrime wing (complaint.nr3c.gov.pk, helpline 1991). Reports build the case that gets mule accounts frozen — including, sometimes, yours.
  5. Change the passwords of any account involved in the payment, from a clean device.

The second scam that follows the first

In the days after, expect messages from "recovery agents" or "cyber experts" who promise to retrieve your money — for a fee, or for your account details. Every one of these is a second scam aimed at the same victim. Real recoveries happen through your bank and the police report, never through strangers who contact you first.
