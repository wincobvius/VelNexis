My iPhone storage is almost full

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 3, 2026 · 3 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 3, 2026

Your iPhone constantly shows "storage almost full" — apps refuse to update, photos stop saving, and the camera lags. iPhones come with fixed storage (64/128/256 GB) with no memory card slot, so cleaning is the only way. The space is usually eaten by videos, message attachments and apps' hidden data — and iPhone Storage settings show it all.

The map first

  1. Settings → General → iPhone Storage — the colored bar and the per-app list, sorted by size. Do not guess; look. Everyone is surprised by something different: videos, podcasts, an app's "Documents & Data", or photos.

The big wins, in order of typical size

  1. Review the recommendations at the top — Apple suggests offloading unused apps, reviewing large attachments, and emptying recently-deleted photos. "Offload Unused Apps" removes the app but keeps its documents — reinstall returns everything.
  2. Messages attachments: the storage page shows Messages' size — open Messages → Settings (in-app) → review large attachments; years of group-photo videos hide there. Old conversations auto-clean if you set "Keep Messages → 1 Year".
  3. Photos: enable iCloud Photos with "Optimize iPhone Storage" (Settings → Photos) — full-res copies live in the cloud, space-saving versions on the phone. And empty the "Recently Deleted" album; deleted photos hold their space for 30 days otherwise.
  4. Apps you never open: delete outright (re-download anytime — your data is in the app's account for anything that has login). "Offload" the maybes.
  5. Music and podcasts downloaded for offline — a full library download is quietly tens of GB. Keep favorites, stream the rest.

The realistic floor

After cleanup, keep 10-15% free permanently — iPhones misbehave below that (camera lag, update failures, exactly the symptoms that sent you here). And if the phone is genuinely full of things you need, the next phone's storage tier is the honest answer — 64 GB dies young in the video era.
