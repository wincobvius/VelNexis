My bank transfer has not arrived and it has been many days

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 12, 2026 · 3 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 12, 2026

Someone sent you money — or you sent it to family — and it has not arrived. Days pass, everyone checks their account daily, and frustration builds. International transfers in particular have several honest delay points; knowing the real timelines and the escalation ladder turns helpless waiting into concrete action.

Know the normal timelines first

  • Local/same-bank transfers: usually minutes to hours.
  • Domestic transfers between banks: typically same day to one working day.
  • International SWIFT transfers: 3-7 working days is normal — and the money can pass through 2-3 intermediary banks on the way, each adding its own processing day. "Many days" starts counting after that window.

The escalation ladder

  1. Get the reference number from the sender — the transfer receipt's tracking number (SWIFT: the MT103 reference). Without it, neither bank can trace anything.
  2. Ask the sender to request a payment trace from their bank. The sending bank can follow the money through the chain and see exactly where it sits — this is the single most effective step.
  3. Verify the account details were correct. A wrong IBAN or account number means the money is not lost — it is slowly bouncing back to the sender, which can take additional days. Check character by character.
  4. Recipient side: confirm with your own bank that no incoming transfer is sitting in a hold or review.
  5. Past 10 working days? File a formal complaint with both banks (they are obliged to respond within regulator-set timeframes), and use the reference numbers and complaint IDs as your paper trail.

The realistic outcome

The overwhelming majority of "lost" transfers turn out to be delayed at an intermediary or slowly returning from a wrong account number — and do arrive, or return to the sender, once traced.
