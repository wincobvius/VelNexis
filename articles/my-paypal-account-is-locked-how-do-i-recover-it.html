My PayPal account is locked, how do I recover it

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 4, 2026 · 3 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 4, 2026

You try to log in and PayPal tells you your account is "limited" or locked. Take a breath — this is usually recoverable, and PayPal tells you exactly what it wants. A limit means PayPal has paused some or all account activity pending verification: typically identity documents, proof of address, or clarification of a transaction. The reasons range from routine (a regulatory check, unusual login location) to serious (a dispute or a violated policy) — and the recovery path runs through one place.

The recovery path

  1. Read the email PayPal sent — it states the reason category for the limit. Read it carefully; your next steps depend on which reason they name.
  2. Log in and open the Resolution Center. Even a limited account can usually open this page, and it lists the exact required steps as a checklist: often photo ID, a proof of address (utility bill, bank statement), and sometimes confirmation of a specific transaction.
  3. Upload clear, valid documents — full document, all corners visible, not expired, name matching the account. Fuzzy or mismatched uploads are the number one reason recoveries drag on.
  4. Wait for the review — typically a few days. Answer any follow-up only through the official site, never through email links.
  5. If the Resolution Center shows steps you believe are wrong or already completed, contact PayPal support through the Help Center and ask for the specific blocker. A polite, specific appeal with clean documents resolves most disputes.

The one mistake to avoid

Never create a second account while one is limited. It feels like a workaround; it is a policy violation that links and limits both accounts, and can permanently burn your identity and bank details on the platform.
