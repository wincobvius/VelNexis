Paper keeps jamming in my printer again and again

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 10, 2026 · 3 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 10, 2026

Every print job becomes a paper jam — you clear one, and the next page jams too. Repeated jams mean something systematic: how the paper is loaded, the paper's quality, debris left from old jams, or worn rollers. Fixing the pattern, not just the jam, ends the cycle.

Load it right (the systematic check)

  1. Fan, square, seat: fan the stack, square it against the tray's edge guides (guides TOUCHING the paper — loose guides skew the feed angle into the first roller), and never above the max-fill line. Skewed entry is the #1 self-inflicted jam.
  2. The paper itself: damp or cheap paper jams far more than fresh 75-80gsm. A ream left open in humid months feeds terribly — try a fresh ream before blaming the machine. And do not re-feed just-jammed paper: it is curled and creased, and jams again — feeding a jammed sheet back in is how one jam becomes five.

Clear the debris (the invisible jam)

  1. Find the remains of old jams: open every panel (rear door especially — most people only look in front) with the printer OFF, and remove every torn scrap. A corner of paper left from the last jam hits every next sheet. A torch helps; rotate the rollers by hand to see the whole path.
  2. Objects in the path: staples, paper clips, a pen cap from an "it fell in there" moment — a torch down the tray slot reveals them.

The roller reality

  1. Clean the rollers: wipe the pickup roller and the internal feed rollers with a barely-damp lint-free cloth. Dust-glazed rubber slips, and slipping = skewing = jamming.
  2. The age verdict: rollers polished shiny and a printer past a few thousand pages — rollers are consumables. Replacement kits are cheap and the swap is 10-15 minutes with the maker's service manual (public on their sites). Jam rate drops to zero afterwards — the actual end of the cycle.

Labels and envelopes — the specialist jams

Only through the manual feed/bypass tray if your printer has one, correct label paper, never re-used labels (they peel inside and lodge). Envelopes: flat, uncurled, flap-side as the manual specifies.
