How do I clear cache and cookies in my browser

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 15, 2026 · 2 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 15, 2026

"Clear your cache and cookies" is the standard advice for half of all browser problems — logins misbehaving, old pages loading, broken layouts. Many people avoid it because they fear losing saved passwords or bookmarks. Here is exactly what it does and does not remove.

What you are clearing

  • Cache — saved copies of pages, so sites load faster. Clearing it makes the first revisit to each site slower, once.
  • Cookies — login sessions and site preferences. Clearing them signs you out of most sites.

Passwords, bookmarks, history and extensions are separate boxes — untouched unless you tick them.

In Chrome, Edge or Firefox

  1. Press Ctrl + Shift + Delete (same shortcut in all three).
  2. Set the time range — "All time" for a full clean.
  3. Tick "Cookies and other site data" and "Cached images and files".
  4. Leave "Passwords" and "Autofill" unticked.
  5. Click Clear data, then restart the browser.

The targeted alternative

If only one site misbehaves, clear just that site: click the padlock → Site settings → Delete data. You stay logged in everywhere else.

Will I lose my saved passwords?

No — unless you deliberately tick the Passwords box. It is unticked by default in every major browser.

Why does the browser feel slower afterwards?

It is re-downloading everything it had cached. That passes after one visit per site.

Quick recap

  • Ctrl+Shift+Del opens the clear-data window on any desktop browser.
  • Clear 'cached images' freely, but clearing cookies logs you out everywhere - do it on purpose. (Curious what a cache actually is? Mozilla's documentation explains it properly: HTTP caching at MDN.)
  • To fix one misbehaving site only, clear its data via the padlock icon > site settings.
  • Android Chrome keeps this under Settings > Privacy > Clear browsing data.
