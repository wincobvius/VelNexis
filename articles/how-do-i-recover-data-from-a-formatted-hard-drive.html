How do I recover data from a formatted hard drive

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 13, 2026 · 3 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 13, 2026

A drive got formatted — an accident, a wrong click, a technician moving too fast — and everything on it seems gone. Here is the technical reality that matters: a normal "quick" format removes the drive's index, not the files themselves. The pages are still there; the table of contents was deleted. Recovery software rebuilds its own contents page and reads the pages directly — which is why what you do in the next hour decides the outcome.

Before you start: Recovery only works until something overwrites the old files. Do not install any software on that drive, do not save anything to it, and always recover files to a different drive.

The recovery process

  1. Stop using the drive immediately. Every file written after the format can overwrite a lost one. If it is your system drive, shut the machine down and work from another computer.
  2. Scan it with a recovery tool. Recuva (wizard-driven, Windows) and PhotoRec (free, every platform, less friendly) both do deep scans that find files the index no longer mentions.
  3. Recover to a different drive — the tool insists on this for good reason. Recovering onto the same drive overwrites the very files being recovered.
  4. Expect partial results. Files that were already fragmented, or areas overwritten since the format, may come back corrupted or not at all. Recover first, then judge.

When chances drop

A full format (Windows unchecks "Quick Format") writes over the drive, making recovery far harder. And a drive that was actively used after formatting loses files to overwriting every day. If the data is genuinely irreplaceable — business records, family archives — a professional data-recovery service costs more but works from a stronger position than any home attempt.
