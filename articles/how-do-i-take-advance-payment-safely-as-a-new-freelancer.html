How do I take advance payment safely as a new freelancer

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 29, 2026 · 3 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 29, 2026

You are a new freelancer with no reviews, and clients ask "why should I pay in advance?" — while you are equally afraid of doing work and getting nothing. The advance system protects both sides when it is set up properly. New freelancers lose money mostly because they deliver everything first and hope.

The structures that protect both sides

  1. Milestones — the industry standard: split the project into 2-4 stages, each with its own payment. A website: design approval (30%), build (40%), launch (30%). The client risks little at any moment; you are never more than one milestone of unpaid work deep. Upwork and Fiverr have built-in milestone/escrow systems — USE them rather than promising custom work on chat.
  2. Escrow where platforms exist: on Upwork, funded milestones hold the client's money before you work. On Fiverr, the order payment is held until delivery. Direct clients without a platform: Wise/Payoneer request the deposit before work begins.
  3. A written scope, always: one page — what you deliver, by when, how many revisions, what costs extra. Advances collapse into disputes when the scope was vibes. A clear "this includes X revisions; more costs Y" prevents the free-work spiral.
  4. The deposit amount: 30-50% upfront for new client relationships is normal and expected. Small projects can be 100% upfront — the client's risk is small, and your risk of unpaid work is entire.

Handling the fear on both sides

  1. When a client refuses any advance: offer a paid small first task ($20-50) instead — a trial that pays. Clients who refuse even a paid trial are telling you something; believe them.
  2. When YOU fear asking: frame it as process, not distrust — "I take 40% to start, balance on delivery — standard for my projects." Confidence is contagious; apology invites negotiation.
  3. Never deliver final files before final payment: watermarks on drafts, staged handover of originals. The finished work is your leverage — releasing it unpaid is volunteering.
