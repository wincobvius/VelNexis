How do I recover photos from a corrupted SD card

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Jul 28, 2026 · 2 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Jul 28, 2026

Your camera or phone memory card suddenly shows "corrupted", "unsupported", or asks to format — and it holds months of photos you never backed up. SD cards corrupt from sudden removal, power cuts during writing, cheap card quality, and plain old age. The photos are usually still physically on the card; the card's index just broke. Recovery software reads around the broken index.

How to fix it

  1. Stop using the card. Do not format it even if the phone asks.
  2. Put it in a card reader on a computer.
  3. If it opens, copy photos out immediately.
  4. If it does not open, use Recuva or PhotoRec to scan it — PhotoRec recovers from badly broken cards.
  5. Recover to the computer, never back to the same card.

Do this before anything else

  • Stop using the card right away. New photos can overwrite the old ones - that is the one mistake you cannot undo.
  • Do not format it, even if the phone or PC asks you to. Formatting makes recovery harder.
  • Put the card in a card reader on a computer. If Windows offers to check and repair the drive, click yes - this often fixes the file system and the photos appear again.
  • If that fails, use a free recovery tool like Recuva (Windows) or PhotoRec (any system). Save recovered files to the computer, never back to the same card.
  • If the card is not detected at all, try a different reader or a different computer before assuming it is dead.
  • Recovery tools cannot promise everything back. The more the card was used after the corruption, the less comes back.
