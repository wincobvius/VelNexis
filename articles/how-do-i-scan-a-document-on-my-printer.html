How do I scan a document on my printer

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 30, 2026 · 2 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 30, 2026

You need to scan a document — for an online form, an office requirement, an ID submission — and want it done properly the first time. Every all-in-one printer scans, and the decisions are simple: glass or feeder, PDF or photo, color or black. The scan lands in your Documents folder by default.

How to fix it

  1. Put the document face down on the glass (look for the arrow mark), or face up in the sheet feeder.
  2. On the computer, open the printer's software, or use Windows "Windows Scan" app / "Fax and Scan".
  3. Choose scan type: document or photo, color or black.
  4. Choose PDF for documents, JPG for photos.
  5. Click Scan and find the file in Documents or the app's save folder.

The easiest ways to scan

  • On Windows, open the free 'Windows Scan' app from the Microsoft Store, press Scan, and save as PDF. It works with almost every printer that has a scanner.
  • Your printer brand's own app (HP Smart, Canon PRINT, Epson Smart Panel) also scans and has simple presets for photo versus document.
  • No computer? Your phone is a scanner: Google Drive > plus button > Scan. It crops, straightens, and saves as PDF for free.
  • Put the page face down on the glass, close the lid, and keep the glass clean - dust lines are the most common scan problem.
  • Scan important documents at 300 DPI. That is sharp enough for forms and IDs without making huge files.
  • Use the paper tray on top (ADF) for multi-page documents - it scans all pages into one PDF automatically.

Scan a page with a signature or stamp at 300 DPI minimum - lower settings blur the ink into pixels, and banks reject them. It is the one scan setting genuinely worth knowing.
