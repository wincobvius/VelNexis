PayPal is not available in Pakistan, what can I use instead

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 3, 2026 · 3 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 3, 2026

Every international client asks "Can I pay via PayPal?" — and the answer in Pakistan has been "no" for years, because PayPal never launched here. People lose clients over this, and some try risky tricks like fake foreign accounts, which end in frozen funds. The better move: know the legal alternatives and how to present them professionally.

What to offer instead

  1. Wise — the closest PayPal-like experience for clients: they pay a local transfer in their own currency, you receive PKR in your Pakistani bank. Cheapest overall for most invoices.
  2. Payoneer — send the client a payment request; they pay by card or bank transfer. Universally recognized by companies that pay freelancers.
  3. Direct SWIFT transfer — for bigger invoices and corporate clients: your bank's IBAN receives the remittance through official channels.
  4. Crypto — know the rules first: some clients offer stablecoins; acceptance is your call, but Pakistan's regulatory posture on crypto has been restrictive and evolving — treat it as a compliance question, not a default, and prefer documented channels for business income.

The professional script (this is the real fix)

  1. Answer the payment question before it kills the deal: "PayPal isn't available here — I accept Wise, Payoneer, or direct bank transfer, whichever is easiest for you." Calm, one line, three options. Clients pay freelancers through unfamiliar methods all the time; the sentence is the whole skill.

The traps that freeze money

  • Fake-foreign PayPal accounts (VPN + foreign address + virtual card): PayPal's verification eventually asks for that country's ID and bank — the account and its balance freeze. Freelancers lose real earnings this way every month.
  • Asking a relative abroad to receive payments mixes personal and business money, hits their tax situation, and breaks most platforms' rules for marketplace clients.
  • "PayPal agents" who convert money for a fee: the classic gray-market trap — money laundering risk lands on YOU.
