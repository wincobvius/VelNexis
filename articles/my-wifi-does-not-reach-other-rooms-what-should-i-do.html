My WiFi does not reach other rooms, what should I do

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 3, 2026 · 2 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 3, 2026

The internet is fast near the router but weak or dead in bedrooms, the upper floor or the courtyard. WiFi signals are blocked by walls, ceilings, metal cupboards and even fridges, so one router often cannot cover a whole house. The fix can be free (moving the router) or cheap (an extender) or proper (a mesh system), depending on the house size.

How to fix it

  1. Move the router to a central, open, high place.
  2. Keep it away from metal cupboards, mirrors and thick walls.
  3. Buy a WiFi range extender and place it halfway between router and the weak room.
  4. Or use a mesh WiFi system for big houses.
  5. Or run a long network cable to the other room and add a second small router there.

Stretch your WiFi properly

  • Move the router to the center of the house, high up, away from mirrors, metal cabinets and microwaves. Placement changes more than any setting.
  • For far rooms, join the 2.4 GHz network - it is slower but reaches much further than 5 GHz.
  • Change the router channel if neighbors' WiFi crowds yours (use 1, 6 or 11 on 2.4 GHz). Free WiFi analyzer apps show how crowded each channel is.
  • A cheap WiFi repeater placed halfway between the router and the dead room covers one room well.
  • For a full house, a mesh kit (two or three units) is the real fix - one router cannot push signal through many walls.
  • Powerline adapters send internet through your electric wiring and work great for one far room with bad WiFi.

Walk the house with a WiFi analyzer app and note where the signal drops. The map shows exactly where a repeater belongs - halfway through the drop zone, not inside the dead room itself.
