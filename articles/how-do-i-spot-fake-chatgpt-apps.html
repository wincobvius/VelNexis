How do I spot fake ChatGPT apps

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 22, 2026 · 2 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 22, 2026

You search "ChatGPT" in an app store and find dozens of apps with near-identical names and logos. Some charge subscriptions, some ask for card details for a "free trial," some promise unlimited access to top models free forever. A few are misleading apps built to harvest logins and card numbers.

How to stay on the safe side

  1. Prefer the official source: OpenAI's own website, and its official app listing on the App Store or Play Store. Check the developer name on the listing page — not just the icon.
  2. Be skeptical of paywalls and "free forever" promises around a product that already has its own official free tier. An app demanding your card before you can try anything is a warning sign on its own.
  3. A different developer name does not automatically mean malware — but it does mean you are not using the official product, and reviews mentioning stolen money or refused refunds deserve real weight.
  4. Never reuse a password in such apps that you use anywhere important.

If you already installed one

Uninstall it, change the password you used there (and everywhere you reused it), and check your card statements for unfamiliar charges — dispute anything wrong with your bank.

Quick recap

  • Install only from the official Play Store or App Store, and check the developer name matches OpenAI.
  • A 'free ChatGPT Plus' app or a clone promising no limits is fake - always.
  • Real AI apps never ask for SMS permissions or contacts access.
  • Fake clones steal your login - if you typed your password into one, change it now.

Check the developer name before installing - the official app comes from OpenAI only. Anything with a similar logo, extra promises, or a subscription price that differs from the official one is a copy built to farm logins.
