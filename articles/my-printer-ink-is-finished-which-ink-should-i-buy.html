My printer ink is finished, which ink should I buy

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 24, 2026 · 2 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 24, 2026

Your printer ran dry and you are standing in the market (or an online store) confused by dozens of cartridge numbers, original versus compatible options, and wildly different prices. Buying the wrong cartridge is a common, avoidable loss. The cartridge number is printed in specific places, and the original-vs-copy choice has honest trade-offs.

How to fix it

  1. Check the exact cartridge number printed on the old cartridge or in the manual.
  2. Buy that exact number — similar-looking ones do not fit.
  3. Original ink is best for the printer's life; compatible (copy) ink is cheaper but can cause head problems.
  4. For ink tank printers, use the brand's own ink bottles — wrong ink clogs heads.
  5. Buy from a trusted shop — fake cartridges are common.

Pick the right ink

  • Find the exact model number on the printer itself, usually on the front or top - not the cartridge number. Model first, then ink.
  • Search 'ink for [exact model]' and cross-check that the cartridge code in the results matches what is in your printer now.
  • Original cartridges cost more but rarely cause errors. Good third-party ones work fine - read recent reviews about chip errors before buying.
  • Check the page yield (how many pages it prints). A cheap cartridge with low yield often costs more per page than the original.
  • For heavy printing, an ink tank printer with bottles costs far less per page than cartridges - worth remembering at your next purchase.
  • Keep one spare cartridge sealed at home. Ink always finishes at the worst time.

Check the page yield number on the box, not the price. Two cartridges with the same price can print wildly different page counts, and the higher-yield one is almost always the cheaper choice per page.
