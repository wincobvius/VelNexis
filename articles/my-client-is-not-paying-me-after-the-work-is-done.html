My client is not paying me after the work is done

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 27, 2026 · 2 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 27, 2026

You delivered the work — design, writing, development, whatever was agreed — and now the client has gone quiet, keeps saying "next week", or refuses outright. This is the freelancer's oldest problem. Your recovery options depend on how the deal was made: platform-protected (Fiverr/Upwork), direct with contract, or informal via chat. Each path has its own leverage.

How to fix it

  1. If the work was through Fiverr/Upwork, the money is protected — never work outside the platform for a new client.
  2. For direct clients: send a polite payment reminder with the invoice.
  3. Keep all proof: chats, emails, delivered files, agreed price.
  4. If they still refuse and the amount is big, legal notice through a lawyer works in many cases.
  5. Lesson for next time: always take 30–50% advance before starting.

Get your payment professionally

  • Send one polite reminder with the invoice attached and a clear deadline. Many late payments are disorganization, not bad intent.
  • Check what the client approved in writing - chat logs, emails and scope approvals are your evidence.
  • Stop further work until the invoice clears. Continuing after non-payment teaches the client that deadlines do not matter.
  • Offer a payment plan if they claim cash flow problems - half now, half in two weeks, in writing.
  • For international clients, confirm their payment gateway details (Payoneer, Wise) - sometimes money is simply stuck on their side.
  • Never hand over final source files or admin access before final payment - that is your last leverage.
  • For larger amounts, a legal notice through a lawyer is cheap and often works.

For your next project, take 50 percent upfront as policy. Clients who refuse a deposit are telling you something important before the work starts, and hearing it early is cheaper than hearing it at invoice time.
