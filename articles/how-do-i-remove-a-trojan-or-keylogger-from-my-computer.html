How do I remove a trojan or keylogger from my computer

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 24, 2026 · 3 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 24, 2026

A scan flagged a trojan — harmful software hiding inside a normal-looking program — or you suspect a keylogger, its worst form: it records every key you press, including bank passwords and private messages. These infections usually arrive with cracked software, "free" versions of paid tools, and fake downloads. Removal needs to be deliberate, because these programs actively resist normal cleanup.

The removal sequence

  1. Disconnect the internet first — unplug the cable or turn off Wi-Fi. This stops the malware communicating, uploading what it has collected, or downloading reinforcements.
  2. Boot into Safe Mode: hold Shift while clicking Restart → Troubleshoot → Advanced options → Startup Settings → Restart → Safe Mode. Most malware does not load in Safe Mode.
  3. Scan with Windows Security (Virus and threat protection → Full scan), then run a second scan with the free version of Malwarebytes. Two scanners catch what one misses; trojans frequently disable or hide from a single tool.
  4. Uninstall unknown programs: Settings → Apps, sorted by install date. Remove anything unfamiliar that appeared around the time the problems started.
  5. Clean up browser extensions too — trojans often install an extension that survives the main cleanup.

The step everyone forgets

Change your passwords — from a different, clean device. If a keylogger ran on this PC, every password typed on it may be recorded, including the new ones you are about to set. Bank and email passwords come first. Then turn on two-factor authentication everywhere important.

If the infection survives all this

A full Windows reset ("Remove everything") is the certain fix — back up documents (not programs!) first. For a machine with banking activity on it, a professional cleanup is worth the cost for certainty.
