I entered my card details on a fake website, what should I do

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Jul 26, 2026 · 2 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Jul 26, 2026

You paid on a site, entered your card details — and now realize it was fake. The order never arrived, the site vanished, or the "bank verification" page was a scam. Your card details are in a criminal's hands, and unauthorized charges can follow within hours. This is a banking emergency, and the first phone call matters more than anything you will do afterwards.

The order of operations

  1. Call your bank's helpline right now — the number on the back of your card or the bank's official app, never a number from the fake site. Ask them to block the card and note the incident. This single call ends most of the risk.
  2. Ask about disputed transactions. If a charge already went through, tell the bank it was fraud — banks have a process for reversing unauthorized transactions, and reporting quickly strengthens your case.
  3. Change passwords for any accounts connected to that site. If you created an account with a reused password on the scam site, that password is now burned — change it everywhere.
  4. Watch your account activity daily for a few weeks. Small "test" charges sometimes precede large ones. Report anything unfamiliar immediately.
  5. Report the fake site: FIA cybercrime (complaint.nr3c.gov.pk, 1991) in Pakistan. Reports help take these sites down for the next person.

What not to expect

Recovery is likely when reported fast, but never guaranteed — which is exactly why step 1 cannot wait for "morning".

Quick recap

  • Freeze or block the card in your bank app right now - this is the single most important minute.
  • Watch statements for weeks and enable transaction alerts.
  • Change the password you reused on that fake page, everywhere you reused it.
  • Report the site to your bank and mark it as phishing in the browser - it protects the next person.
