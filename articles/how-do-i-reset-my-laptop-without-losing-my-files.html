How do I reset my laptop without losing my files

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 8, 2026 · 3 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 8, 2026

Your laptop has become unusable — too slow, too many errors, software problems everywhere — and the cleanest fix is a reset. But a normal reset wipes everything, and your photos, documents and work files are on it. Windows has a special reset option that removes all programs and settings but keeps your personal files.

Before you touch anything

  1. Back up anyway — this is the rule, not a suggestion: "Keep my files" keeps files on the system drive's user folders (Desktop, Documents, Pictures...), but no reset is a substitute for a backup. Copy your folders to a USB drive or cloud first — 30 minutes now versus a bad surprise later. Files on OTHER drives (D:, E:) are untouched by resets regardless.

The reset, step by step

  1. Settings → System → Recovery → Reset this PC → Reset.
  2. Choose "Keep my files" — NOT "Remove everything". This is the entire trick: Windows reinstalls itself cleanly, removes ALL installed programs (it will show you the list), resets settings — but your personal files in user folders survive.
  3. Choose "Local reinstall" or "Cloud download": local reinstalls from the machine's own copy (faster, fine for most); cloud download fetches a fresh Windows (cleaner, needs internet and ~4 GB of data). For a laptop that is sick from software rot, cloud download is the more thorough medicine.
  4. Let it run — 30-90 minutes, plugged in, untouched. The laptop restarts several times; that is the process, not a problem.

After the reset

  1. Reinstall your programs from the saved list (the reset gives you an HTML list of removed apps on the desktop) — from official sources only.
  2. Windows Update immediately — a fresh install plus pending updates is the stable baseline.
  3. Re-check your files: Documents, Desktop, Pictures present and openable? The backup from step 1 is your safety copy either way.

If even the reset fails or the problem returns

A reset that changes nothing points at hardware (drive, RAM) rather than Windows — and a drive that is failing can die during a reset, which is why step 1 exists. For machines that reset fine but rot again within months: the habits that caused it (cleaners, sketchy downloads, 40 startup programs) reinstall themselves — see our slow-PC guide for the permanent lifestyle.
