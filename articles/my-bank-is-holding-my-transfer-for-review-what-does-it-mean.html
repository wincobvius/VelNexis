My bank is holding my transfer for review, what does it mean

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 31, 2026 · 3 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 31, 2026

You sent a transfer and instead of arriving, it sits marked "under review" or "pending verification". Before assuming the worst: this is usually routine. Banks are required to check unusual transfers for fraud and money laundering, and most reviews clear on their own. The frustration is real, but the process has logic — and you can speed it up.

What triggers a review

  • Large amounts relative to your usual activity
  • First-time international transfers, or transfers to countries your account has never dealt with
  • Rapid sequences of transfers, or patterns that resemble fraud automation
  • Incomplete or mismatched recipient details

None of these mean the bank suspects you specifically — they are pattern checks required of the bank, and every customer passes through them occasionally.

What actually helps

  1. Answer the bank's verification call — but only from the bank's official number. If you miss it, call back on the helpline printed on your card; never on a number left in a voicemail (that is a known scam pattern).
  2. Have your documentation ready: the invoice, admission letter, family-support reason, or contract behind the payment. A clear purpose stated up front shortens most reviews dramatically.
  3. Ask for a timeline and a reference number for the review. Reviews typically clear within 1-3 working days; a reference number makes follow-up concrete.
  4. Past the promised time? Visit the branch with your documents and the reference number, and ask for the compliance contact handling the case.

One thing worth knowing

The bank cannot legally tell you much about the receiving side of a review — that silence is regulation, not stonewalling. What you control is your side: responsive, documented, and patient.
