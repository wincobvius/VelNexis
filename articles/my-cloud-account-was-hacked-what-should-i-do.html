My cloud account was hacked, what should I do

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Sep 4, 2026 · 3 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Sep 4, 2026

Your Google, iCloud or Dropbox account shows signs of a stranger: unknown logins, missing files, shared links you never made, password reset emails you did not request. Cloud accounts hold your entire digital life — documents, photos, backups — so the cleanup must be fast and thorough, in the right order.

The order matters

  1. Change the password immediately — from a device you trust. myaccount.google.com, appleid.apple.com, or dropbox.com's account page. The new password exists nowhere else.
  2. Sign out every other session. Each service has its version: Google's "Your devices," Apple's device list, Dropbox's connected devices. Password change alone does not evict an already-signed-in intruder — this step does.
  3. Turn on two-factor authentication if it was off. This is the lock that makes the difference between "one bad afternoon" and "recurring nightmare."

The audit — what they did

  1. Recent activity: sign-in history, file activity. What was viewed, downloaded, or deleted? (Google Workspace users: the admin can see Drive activity in detail.)
  2. Sharing changes: the quiet, profitable hack is not deleting files — it is sharing your private folders outward. Review sharing on your folders and revoke anything you did not create.
  3. Recovery details: recovery email and phone still yours? Intruders change these to keep a back door open. Fix them first if touched.
  4. Connected apps and third-party access: revoke everything unfamiliar.

The aftermath

Files deleted by an intruder may sit in the cloud Trash — restore fast, before retention expires (see our Drive recovery guide for the windows). And if business or client data was exposed, document what happened and notify whoever is affected — speed and honesty are also the professional move.
