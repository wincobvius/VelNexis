I got a call saying I won a prize, is it a scam

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 13, 2026 · 2 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 13, 2026

An unknown caller announces that you have won a lottery, a prize, a lucky draw — maybe "from your mobile company" or a big brand. To claim it, they need a small "registration fee", your bank details, or an OTP. It feels exciting for exactly the seconds it takes to remember two facts: you cannot win a draw you never entered, and real prizes never ask winners to pay.

What to do on the call

  1. Say nothing that confirms who you are — no name confirmation, no "yes" to leading questions. Scammers record small confirmations and reuse them.
  2. Do not pay any "fee". Processing fees, registration fees, tax-before-delivery — every version of pay-first-to-receive-later is the same scam.
  3. Do not share an OTP or card details, ever. No real company asks for these on a call. An OTP is the key to your account; reading it out is handing over the key.
  4. Hang up and block the number. You owe a scammer no politeness.
  5. Report it: PTA's spam-reporting channel for the number, and FIA cybercrime (1991) if you shared anything.

Protect the people around you

These calls target elders disproportionately — the "prize" story was built for them. Tell your parents and grandparents the two-sentence version: you cannot win what you did not enter, and anyone asking for a fee or OTP to release a prize is a scammer.

Quick recap

  • A real prize never asks you to pay anything first - any 'fee' means scam.
  • They rush you and ask for secrecy - both are manipulation, not procedure.
  • Never share the OTP they ask for - that OTP empties your account.
  • Hang up, block, and report the number; telling family protects them from the same call.
