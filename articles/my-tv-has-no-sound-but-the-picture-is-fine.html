My TV has no sound but the picture is fine

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 21, 2026 · 3 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 21, 2026

Your TV shows a perfect picture but produces no sound — silence from apps, TV channels, everything. The sound path in modern TVs has many exits: internal speakers, HDMI ARC, optical, Bluetooth, headphones. The TV is often sending sound somewhere else while you listen to nothing. Finding where the sound went is the whole fix.

Find where the sound went

  1. Press the volume button and read the TV screen. A small on-screen bar shows WHERE audio is heading — "Bluetooth: [device name]", "HDMI/ARC", "Optical", or nothing (internal speakers). If it names a device you are not using, the sound is flowing there: switch the audio output to TV Speaker (Settings → Sound → Sound Output).
  2. Headphone ghost: a half-inserted 3.5mm cable, or a pair of Bluetooth headphones still paired from last night, mutes the speakers. Unplug the jack / disconnect the Bluetooth — silence ends instantly.
  3. Silence on ONE source only (HDMI box, console, one app) → the problem is that source's audio settings: check the device's own audio output format (set to PCM or Auto rather than bitstream-only when compatibility is in question), and try the HDMI cable — a cheap cable carries a picture but drops audio more often than people expect.

The deeper checks

  1. Every app, every channel silent: check Settings → Sound for an "expert" audio mode gone wrong — reset the sound settings to default. Then the full power cycle (unplug a minute) — TV audio services crash like any other software.
  2. The factory reset as the software last resort: Settings → Support → Reset — a working-before, silent-after software fault dies here.

The hardware verdict

If a reset changes nothing and a soundbar/headphones work when plugged in, the TV's internal speakers or audio board have failed. External speakers are the affordable workaround (a soundbar via HDMI ARC or optical), and a repair quote tells you whether the board is worth it — on cheaper TVs, the soundbar usually wins.
