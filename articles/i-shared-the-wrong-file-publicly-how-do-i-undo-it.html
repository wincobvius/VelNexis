I shared the wrong file publicly, how do I undo it

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 28, 2026 · 3 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 28, 2026

You shared a file "anyone with the link" — and then realized it was the wrong file: private photos, salary documents, client data. Panic is natural; the fix is fast. Changing sharing settings cuts off the link instantly. Anyone who has the link loses access the moment you flip the switch.

Cut off the link now

  1. Right-click the file → Share (or the person-link icon).
  2. Change "Anyone with the link" to "Restricted." The old link dies the moment you click Done — clicks on it now meet a request-for-access wall that comes to YOU. Watch for access requests in the next days: they reveal exactly who holds the link.
  3. Check "General access" AND the named people — a mistaken share sometimes includes specific emails too. Remove anyone who should not be there.

Damage assessment — the part speed decides

  1. Anyone who ALREADY downloaded or copied the file keeps their copy — no sharing setting reaches files on other people's computers. The link cutoff stops future access, not past downloads. For sensitive documents, this is the honest limit of the fix.
  2. If it was truly sensitive data (IDs, financial records, client information): note when you shared it and when you revoked it, and tell whoever the data belongs to — for a client's files, a fast heads-up is professional; for personal IDs, watch for misuse and report it if it happens.

Prevention

For anything private, "Restricted" is the default you want, and "Anyone with the link" is the exception you choose deliberately. And before sharing a folder, glance at what is inside it — most accidents are folder-level shares of folders nobody audited.
