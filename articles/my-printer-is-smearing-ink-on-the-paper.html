My printer is smearing ink on the paper

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Sep 3, 2026 · 2 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Sep 3, 2026

Fresh prints come out smeared — wet streaks (inkjet) or smudged letters (laser) that ruin documents and stain fingers. Smearing has different roots per technology: inkjets lay wet ink that needs the right paper and time to dry; lasers fuse toner with heat, and failures there mean wrong settings or a tired fuser.

How to fix it

  1. Inkjet: wet prints mean the paper is too glossy for the setting — set the correct paper type in print settings.
  2. Check the print head is not dragging — a misaligned head touches paper. Run "Print Head Alignment" from maintenance.
  3. Let prints dry 1–2 minutes before touching.
  4. Laser: smearing means the fuser is not sealing toner — check the paper type and toner.
  5. Clean the inside with a dry lint-free cloth — stray toner smears.

Stop the smears

  • If the printer sat unused for weeks, the first pages smear while old ink clears - print 5 to 10 pages and most smears fade away.
  • Check the paper: damp or dusty paper smears ink. Open a fresh pack and test.
  • Run the printer's alignment and cleaning cycles from its software.
  • Try a different cartridge - a leaking or badly refilled one smears permanently.
  • On laser printers, a worn drum unit causes repeated smears and ghost images. The drum is replaceable separately on many models.
  • Clean the rollers inside the paper path with a dry cloth - ink builds up there and stamps every page.
  • Match the paper type setting in the printer menu to the paper you actually loaded - wrong settings smear.

Print five blank pages in a row - old ink sitting on the rollers transfers to the next real print. This 'sacrificial printing' clears the path faster than three cleaning cycles.
