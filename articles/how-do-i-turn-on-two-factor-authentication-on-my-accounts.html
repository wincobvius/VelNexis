How do I turn on two factor authentication on my accounts

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 20, 2026 · 2 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 20, 2026

Two-factor authentication (2FA) is the single biggest upgrade you can make to any account: a second lock beyond the password, usually a code from your phone or an app. Even if a hacker obtains your password — through a breach or a phishing page — they still cannot log in without that second factor. Every major service supports it; the only problem is that the setting hides in security menus most people never open.

Where to find it

  • Google: myaccount.google.com → Security → 2-Step Verification
  • Facebook: Settings → Accounts Center → Password and security → Two-factor authentication
  • WhatsApp: Settings → Account → Two-step verification (a PIN of your own choosing)
  • Instagram: Settings → Accounts Center → Password and security → Two-factor authentication
  • Apple ID: Settings → [your name] → Sign-In & Security → Two-Factor Authentication

Choosing the second factor

  1. An authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Authy, or your phone's built-in one) generates codes without any SMS involvement. This is the stronger choice — see why below.
  2. SMS codes are better than nothing, but a determined attacker can sometimes redirect your number (SIM swapping) and receive your codes.
  3. Save the backup codes every service offers when you enable 2FA. Losing your phone with 2FA on and no backup codes is its own recovery ordeal — the codes take one minute to store in a safe place.

One habit to keep

When a 2FA prompt arrives that you did not trigger, treat it as an alarm: someone has your password. Deny the prompt and change that password immediately.

Use an authenticator app rather than SMS where the option exists - app codes cannot be intercepted the way text messages can. Google Authenticator or Authy both take two minutes to set up per account.
