My email shows a login from another country

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 22, 2026 · 2 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 22, 2026

An email arrives: "New sign-in to your account from [country you have never visited]." Your stomach drops. Take the alarm seriously — but verify before panicking, because this alert has a famous false-positive generator.

First, check what it actually says

  1. Open the account's official security page directly — Google: myaccount.google.com → Security → "Your devices" (or "Recent activity" for other services). Do not click buttons inside the alert email itself; fake "security alerts" are a phishing favorite.
  2. Read the device, not just the country. The location shown is derived from the IP address and is often wrong in ordinary ways: your VPN, your mobile network's routing, or a server of an app you use can all appear as a foreign login. If the device name is your own phone or laptop, mystery solved.
  3. A device you do not recognize, or a login at 3 AM your time? Act now: click "Sign out" on that session, and change your password immediately.
  4. Turn on two-step verification if it was not already on — after a scare is when people finally do it.
  5. Check recovery details: your recovery email and phone number are still yours? An intruder's first move is often changing these.

The honest calibration

Nine of these alerts out of ten are your own apps or VPN — but you only know which tenth you are in by opening the security page and looking. That two-minute check is the whole difference between "false alarm" and "they are still inside".

One common false alarm: your phone or an app connecting through a server abroad. Check the device name and browser shown next to the login - 'iPhone Safari' from a foreign IP is often your own VPN or an app, not an intruder.

Most foreign-login alerts are harmless routing, but some are real. This table separates the two in seconds.

Safe or suspicious?

Sign in the alertUsually safeUsually suspicious
Device name matches yours, IP abroadVPN or an app routing trafficUnknown device name and browser
