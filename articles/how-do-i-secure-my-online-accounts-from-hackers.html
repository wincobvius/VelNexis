How do I secure my online accounts from hackers

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 18, 2026 · 2 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 18, 2026

Account hacks feel like wizardry. In reality, hackers rarely "crack" anything — they log in with a password you already use somewhere else, obtained from a data breach or a phishing page. That is why securing accounts is less about cleverness and more about four boring, powerful habits.

The four habits

  1. A different password for every important account. Reuse is the number-one cause of mass account takeover: one leaked site unlocks every account sharing its password. A password manager (built into your phone and browser) makes unique passwords effortless — you only remember the one that unlocks the manager.
  2. Length beats cleverness. 12+ characters. Four random unrelated words are both stronger and easier to remember than a "clever" short password with symbol substitutions.
  3. Turn on two-step verification everywhere — email first. Your email is the master key: whoever controls it can reset every other password. Secure it before anything else.
  4. Never log in through links in emails, SMS or chat. Type the address yourself, or use a saved bookmark. The fake-login-page trick works only when you arrive via the link they sent.

Worth doing today

Check whether your email has appeared in known breaches at haveibeenpwned.com — it takes seconds and tells you exactly which passwords need retiring.

Is a password manager safe? Putting everything in one place sounds risky.

A good manager encrypts your vault with one strong master password — far safer than the realistic alternative, which is reused weak passwords everywhere. Just protect the master password and enable 2FA on the manager itself.

Prioritize by blast radius: email first, then banking, then everything else. Whoever owns your email can reset every other password - so the email password should be unique and locked with two-step verification before you touch anything else.
