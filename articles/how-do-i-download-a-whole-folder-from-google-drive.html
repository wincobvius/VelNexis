How do I download a whole folder from Google Drive

VAbdul Rafay
CS student & tech writer · Aug 30, 2026 · 3 min read ✅ Editor-reviewed📅 Published Aug 30, 2026

You need an entire folder from Google Drive — dozens of files — and downloading them one by one would take all day. Drive downloads whole folders as a single ZIP package automatically. The process is one right-click, with a few practical tricks for very large folders that tend to fail at 95%.

The basic download

  1. Right-click the folder → Download. Drive zips the folder in the cloud and delivers one file. Small folders finish in seconds.
  2. Extract the ZIP on your computer — right-click → Extract All (Windows) or double-click (Mac). Until you extract, you are browsing a compressed snapshot, not your files.

For big folders — where it actually gets tricky

  1. The 95% failure point: folders above roughly 2 GB (and heavy with media) often fail as one ZIP. The fix is splitting: enter the folder, select the subfolders (or groups of files), and download them as separate ZIPs. Five 500 MB downloads succeed where one 3 GB download dies.
  2. Watch for the "zipping" pause — large folders spend minutes preparing before the download starts. Let the tab sit; closing it cancels the compression.
  3. Download limits on shared folders: files shared by others can hit "quota exceeded" errors when the whole internet downloads them at once. The workaround is copying the folder to YOUR Drive ("Make a copy" / "Add shortcut" then "Create a copy") and downloading from your own copy — quota resets and applies separately.

Worth knowing

ZIPs preserve names and structure but lose sharing permissions — anyone you re-share with starts from default. And keep the Drive copy until the extracted files are verified; a failed ZIP is annoying, a deleted original is a disaster.
